**Hola Terrace Crew!** ☀️🍷

Happy Monday. Pull up a lounger, grab your coffee (or whatever’s keeping the weekend at bay), and let’s catch up on what’s been happening along our stretch of coast from Dénia down through Jávea, Calpe and the bits in between.

The weather’s doing its usual November thing, very mild and mostly cloudy this morning around Dénia, about 19°C, with a chance of a light drizzle later but nothing dramatic. Perfect for a terrace afternoon if the sun decides to show its face.

**Calpe’s heartbreak at La Manzanera**

Yesterday the roof of the old Club Social La Manzanera finally gave way. That iconic Ricardo Bofill building right by the sea (you know the one, next to the Muralla Roja) has been slowly crumbling for years. The cover collapsed into rubble with no one inside, thankfully, but locals said it sounded like a small earthquake. The police cordoned it off straight away, people had still been up on the roof the night before for Halloween.

Mayor Ana Sala was down there the same day and said the town is opening a new administrative file today to try and save what’s left “by every means possible.” There’s a long-running fight with the coastal authorities who want it demolished at the town’s expense. It’s a proper piece of Calpe’s bohemian past, restaurant, pools, little jetty, that whole 1970s dream, and it hurts to see another bit of it go. Fingers crossed something can still be rescued.

**Dénia packed the streets for Noche de las Almas**

While the rest of Spain was marking All Saints’ and official mourning after the train tragedy, Dénia decided the kids still needed their fun. The Noche de las Almas was huge, a big parade of Valencian legendary monsters winding through La Vía, María Hervás, Marqués de Campo and ending at Plaza del Consell. Mini-disco, role-play for the teens, photo spots everywhere, and the little ones in full costume. Great community atmosphere and a reminder that life keeps going. 

**DANA recovery still ticking over**

If you were hit by Adam, today is the day the Generalitat aid window opens in Dénia. Up to €6,000 for essential goods and furnishings in your main home (€3,000 for second homes), plus up to €800 a month for up to a year if you had to move out. You can do it online or in person at the town hall or PROP offices. Agricultural help is also starting. Jávea had a rough time too, over 180 emergency calls and 141 people evacuated when the water came through. A quick heavy shower last week flooded parts of Dénia again, so the ground is still pretty soggy in places.

**Other bits worth knowing**

•  Income gap across the Marina Alta is stark: Xàbia tops out around €32k average, Parcent nearer €23k. Only Xàbia and Dénia sit above the Valencian average.

•  Housing rents remain the hot topic in Dénia, the town is still pushing hard to be declared a “zona tensionada” and has threatened to take the regional government to court if it keeps ignoring them.

•  Christmas is creeping in early. Jávea’s big Gift Fayres are back at Salones Carrasco on 8 and 22 November, 120+ stalls, Santa, family stuff, and money going to local kids’ charity. Tapas route in Dénia still running until 22 November if you fancy a wander. La Cubana are bringing their new show to Calpe mid-month, and Benidorm is doing a €520k spruce-up near Levante beach.

A couple of rescues over the weekend too, two hikers lost for nine hours on Montgó and a climber airlifted from Segària, but everyone got home safe.

That’s the round-up from our little corner of the coast. The sun’s trying, the sea is still inviting, and the terrace is open. Until next Monday.
