Pull up a lounger. This one’s from the Red Tape & Tears pile, the bit that happens after the exciting “your residencia has been approved” message, then you suddenly need the actual plastic card in your hand.

The TIE (Tarjeta de Identidad de Extranjero) is that card. Getting it means a trip to the National Police for fingerprints, the infamous toma de huellas. And yes, you need an appointment first. Free, official, and usually about as easy to grab as a table at a popular beach restaurant in August.

Here’s the plain-English version for those of us on the northern Costa Blanca (Alicante province). I’ve kept it practical and as painless as this process ever gets.

**The Clock Starts Ticking**

You have one month to request the card in person. That clock starts from when you enter Spain on a long-stay visa, or from the date your authorization is granted (or takes effect, sometimes tied to your Social Security registration). Miss it and things get messier, so don’t treat it as an “I’ll get round to it” job.

Where You Book (and the Exact Name That Matters)

Go to the official cita previa portal:

https://icp.administracionelectronica.gob.es/icpplus/

Choose your province (Alicante for most of us on this stretch). Then, under the Policía Nacional options, look for something very close to this:

POLICÍA-TOMA DE HUELLAS (EXPEDICIÓN DE TARJETA) INICIAL, RENOVACIÓN, DUPLICADO Y LEY 14/2013

(The wording can shift slightly by province, but it’ll be the fingerprinting/card issuance one. Do not pick anything from the Oficina de Extranjería list by mistake,  you’ll waste the slot.)

Enter your details exactly as they appear on your passport or NIE, pick an office (or “any office” if you’re happy to travel a bit), add a Spanish mobile and email, and grab a slot if one appears. Save the confirmation PDF like it’s the last cold beer in the fridge.

2026 note: In some provinces (including parts of Alicante), a pilot is running where the police automatically assign a percentage of these appointments and send them by SMS/email after your resolution. If nothing lands within a couple of weeks, book it yourself on the portal as normal.

**What You’ll Need on the Day**

•  Completed and signed EX-17 form (download the current version from the Ministry of Inclusion models page — it should reference the newer regulations).

•  Proof you’ve paid the tasa 790 código 012. Generate this only on the official National Police site: https://sede.policia.gob.es/Tasa790_012/. Typical 2026 amounts: around €16 for a first temporary card, a bit more for renewals or long-term. Always check the figure on the form itself.

•  Valid passport (plus visa/entry stamp if relevant) and copies.

•  The resolution granting your authorisation.

•  One recent colour passport photo on a white background.

•  Your appointment confirmation.

•  Recent padrón certificate (many stations want it; get one if yours is more than a few months old).

Everyone in the family needs their own appointment, kids included. You have to show up in person — no one else can do the fingerprints for you.

**When the Portal Says “No Appointments Available”**

Welcome to the club. Slots disappear fast in Alicante province. Check at different times of day (early morning often works), try every office in the province, and keep dated screenshots of the “no hay citas” messages if your one-month deadline is getting close. That evidence can help if you need to explain a delay.

And please — don’t pay anyone who claims they can “get you a cita.” The official appointment is free. Everything else is either a waste of money or worse.

**After the Fingerprints**

You’ll walk out with a resguardo (receipt). The actual card takes a few weeks to be made. Collection is often a separate appointment or by lot number,ask at the fingerprint appointment so you know the local system. The receipt is not a travel document, so plan accordingly if you need to leave Spain before the card arrives.

That’s the current process as of late 2026. Procedures can still vary slightly by office, so read the info page that appears once you’ve selected Alicante and the procedure on the cita previa site.
