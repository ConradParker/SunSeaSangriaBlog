Hola Terrace Crew! The big story this Monday in Northern Costa Blanca news 2026 is how Spain’s fierce wildfires have sent their smoke drifting right over our skies, while down on the ground and in the water life keeps rolling with rescues, turtle nests, low unemployment numbers and the usual summer mix of heat and community updates from Alicante through El Campello, Benidorm, Calpe, Jávea and Dénia.

**Spain’s Wildfires Cast a Long Shadow**

This has been one of the toughest fire seasons in living memory. Blazes in the Madrid and Ávila areas have scorched tens of thousands of hectares — the Ávila fire is being called the largest on record in Spain — forcing tens of thousands of people to evacuate or stay indoors. An earlier fire in Almería claimed twelve lives. Closer to us, the Vall d’Uixó fire in Castellón has burned more than 6,500 hectares and prompted large-scale evacuations.

Yesterday the University of Alicante’s climate lab confirmed what many of us noticed: the smoke plume from those central fires, pushed by west-northwest winds, reached Alicante province. Skies turned whitish and hazy, with a faint burnt smell in places, and particle levels rose. It replaced the Saharan dust we’d had the day before. Local firefighters from Alicante have been sent north to help, and the Valencian Community has mobilised extra resources. Stay sensible if the air feels heavy — vulnerable folk especially.

**Alicante & El Campello: Heat, Smoke and Summer Energy**

Alicante has been dealing with the heatwave leftovers and the new smoke layer. Earlier in the week, red alerts brought beach and park closures plus strong wind warnings. El Campello, meanwhile, threw a big free LOS40 Summer Live party that pulled in more than 12,000 people. The town has also locked in a ten-year waste and beach-cleaning contract focused on sustainability (and picked up an award for it), plus over €800,000 in Diputación support for local commerce vouchers and day-to-day running costs.

**Benidorm: A Dramatic High-Rise Rescue**

Police in Benidorm rescued an eight-year-old autistic boy who was found sitting on an air-conditioning unit on the 21st-floor balcony of a high-rise. His father was arrested for leaving him alone. A smaller vegetation fire earlier in the month was put out the same day it started.

**Calpe: Jobs, Bikes and a Busy Town**

Calpe’s unemployment rate dropped to 6.97 % in June — the lowest in two decades — with more than a thousand new contracts, most of them permanent. The town also picked up the Más Bici 2026 award from La Vuelta for its work supporting cycling tourism and infrastructure. On the water, maritime authorities rescued 43 people (including eight minors) from two pateras off the coast; three skippers were arrested. A traffic dispute in the town centre escalated into an assault that left one local man needing stitches and dental work.

**Jávea: Six Saved When a Boat Sank**

Saturday afternoon near Cala del Portitxol, a motorboat started taking on water and sank. Six people were pulled to safety first by a nearby private boat, then coordinated with Maritime Rescue. Quick thinking all round. Jávea has also received €81,000 to improve air-conditioning in eight public schools before the new term, and Joe Jonas was spotted enjoying a quiet stretch of the coast.

**Dénia: Turtles, Urchins and Castle Concerts**

The loggerhead turtle known as Mona nested again on Les Bovetes beach — one of only four nests recorded this summer in the whole Comunitat Valenciana — laying around 90 eggs. Further along the coast, 1,800 sea urchins were released into the Cap de Sant Antoni marine reserve to help the species recover. Música al Castell has been packing the castle grounds with concerts, and the Marina Alta as a whole hit a record employment high in June with more than 68,000 affiliations.

**A Few More Local Notes**

Benissa is flying drones to tackle illegal dumping, watch for fire risk and improve security, and has launched a campaign encouraging people to register on the padrón. Teulada-Moraira has been handing out fines (up to €1,200) for illegal waste dumping and is opening August swimming courses. Calima dust is finally easing, but the heat is still hanging around in typical July fashion — keep the water bottle close and the shutters half-closed in the middle of the day.

That’s the roundup from Dénia down toward Alicante this week. The coast keeps turning even when the news further inland is tough.
