Pull up a lounger for a minute. The high summer noise has left for another year; the big water parks are winding down, and the Costa Blanca North is in that quiet little stretch it always goes through around now. 

From Dénia through Jávea, Moraira, Calpe and Altea, the light softens, the roads empty, and you can finally hear yourself think (even the summer cicadas are quiet).

**The parks around Benidorm are closing or running weekend-only.** 

{{< image-caption src="/images/synkope.màxima.jpg" >}}{{< /image-caption >}}

Terra Mítica is mostly weekend-only now, with the Egypt, Greece, and Rome zones open, and Horror Nights wrapping up. Aqualandia is weekend-only, Mundomar still has its Halloween stuff running, and Terra Natura starts its family Halloween programme from the 24th. If you fancy one last day of rides or a colour powder party, grab the online tickets while you can. They are quieter and cheaper than in August.

The walks that actually make sense now are the ones that do not involve queues. Montgó Natural Park, sitting right between Dénia and Jávea, is at its best. You can check the[ official park pages](https://parquesnaturales.gva.es/) or the[ Dénia tourist info on Montgó](https://denia.net/en/naturaleza/montgo-natural-park/) before you head up. The heat has dropped enough that the summit trails are doable without melting, or you can stick to the easier circuits through the pines and limestone if you just want the views over the bays and, on a clear day, the outline of Ibiza. Same story with the Peñón de Ifach in Calpe. That classic climb for the 360 views. You need a free reservation these days; limited numbers, so sort it on the[ official Penyal](https://parquesnaturales.gva.es/va/web/pn-penyal-d-ifac) d’Ifach[ page](https://parquesnaturales.gva.es/va/web/pn-penyal-d-ifac) before you go. Start early, wear the correct shoes (not flip-flops), and kids can usually go as far as the tunnel for solid views without the scrambling. Autumn is the sweet spot for both. Clear skies, manageable temperatures, far fewer people than spring or summer.

Markets and food feel more local too. Jávea’s Thursday market in the old town runs as usual. On the 31st the Mercat d’Origen de la Marina Alta is back at the Riurau d’Arnauda with local produce, crafts and a bit of music. It is a proper morning out rather than a tourist rush. Down in Dénia, the XXVIII Ruta de Tapas is underway from 22 October to 22 November, 48 places, tapa plus drink for 4.50 euros. Details and the list of spots are on[ denia.net](https://denia.net/agenda/xxviii-ruta-de-tapas-2/). It is a good excuse to wander the centre and try something new without the August queues.

{{< image-caption src="/images/albir.jpg" >}}Walks on October {{< /image-caption >}}

Calpe is in the middle of its Moros y Cristianos, running 21 to 26 October this year (50th anniversary and now a national tourist interest festival). You can see the full programme on the[ official fiestas site](https://morosycristianos.calpe.es/en/). Dénia is running its Nit de les Ànimes from mid-October into early November, which feels about right for the place.

Hotel occupancy across the Costa Blanca outside Benidorm was still high in the first half of October, around 82 per cent, so pure bargain basement rooms are not everywhere. What you do get is space. Easier parking, shorter waits for tables, quieter beaches you can actually walk on, and the last of the decent park tickets if that is your thing. Villas and apartments often feel more available and better value than peak summer, especially midweek. The sea is still swimmable on the warmer days for a lot of people. The evenings just need a light layer.

{{< image-caption src="/images/whatsapp-image-2026-03-23-at-08.47.46.jpeg" >}}Hotel occupancy across the Costa Blanca {{< /image-caption >}}



This is the stretch a lot of us who live here quietly prefer. The park's closing does not mean the season is over. It means the North can breathe again. The views from Montgó or the Peñón, a market morning, a terrace sangria that does not need booking two weeks out, and the sense that the place belongs to the people who actually drive the N-332 most of the year.

If you are here now or planning a late October escape, this is when it shows its real face. Get out while the light is still good, and enjoy what the Costa Blanca has to offer.

Right, I’m off for my afternoon walk; it’s Friday so I might add in a cheeky beer watching the world go by.
