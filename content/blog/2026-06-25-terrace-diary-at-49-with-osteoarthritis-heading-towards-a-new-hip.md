**Hola Terrace Crew,** 🌞🍹

Thursday 25 June, coffee on the terrace, dog at my feet, and a few honest thoughts swirling around. I’m heading to physiotherapy again this morning, and I wanted to write this down while it’s fresh.

I’ve been told that my grade 2-3 osteoarthritis is likely leading to a new hip at some point. I’m only 49. That number still surprises me when I say it out loud. I always pictured myself as the one bounding up the hills in Javea or chasing sunsets with the dogs well into my 60s and beyond. Reality, as it often does, had other plans.

**The Emotional Side (Because We’re All Human)**

It’s a weird mix of feelings. Part frustration — why now, when we’re finally living this dream life? Part practicality — okay, let’s get informed and sorted. And a big part determination — this doesn’t get to steal the joy from our Costa Blanca chapter. I’ve had a good cry, a few “why me?” moments, and then pulled up the lounger and reminded myself: plenty of people navigate this and still enjoy every bit of sun, sea, and sangria. I plan to be one of them.

**Living with It Right Now in the Marina Alta**

The stiffness and pain are manageable most days with the tools we’re building:

•  Physio sessions (today’s on the calendar) that focus on strength, mobility, and keeping as active as possible.

•  Gentler rhythms — shorter dog walks in the cooler hours, more terrace time with a view that never gets old, and smart pacing so I can still join in the fiestas (Ondara’s Cristo celebrations are happening right now — I’ll soak up the atmosphere at my own speed).

•  The sea and lighter movement help more than I expected. Even a careful paddle or swim eases things.

•  The healthcare has been reassuring so far. I’m learning the system, asking questions, and feeling supported.

The heat doesn’t always help (those warm nights can make joints grumble), but the lifestyle here gives us options that many places don’t — proximity to good care, beautiful flat walks when hills are too much, and a community that understands taking things slower sometimes.

**Why I’m Sharing This**

If you’re in your 40s or 50s dealing with osteoarthritis, facing a possible joint replacement, or just navigating any health bump while building (or enjoying) life abroad — you’re not alone. It can feel scary and isolating, especially when you’ve moved for a fresher start. But there is life — good, rich, sunny life — on the other side of the adjustments.

I want this to be useful: practical tips as I learn them, honest emotions, and reminders that we adapt and keep going. Whether you’re an expat here, thinking of moving, or just following from afar, I hope it helps you feel less alone on your own terrace.

Drop a comment if this resonates, if you’ve been through something similar, or if you have tips for managing osteoarthritis in Spain (best physios, supplements that worked, gentle activities in the area — all welcome). This Crew is stronger together.

I’ll update as I go — next physio insights, decisions about the hip timeline, how we’re keeping the adventures alive. For now, I’m focusing on today: physio, a gentle walk with the dogs later, and gratitude for this beautiful corner of the world that still feels like home.

**Moving (carefully) forward with love,**
