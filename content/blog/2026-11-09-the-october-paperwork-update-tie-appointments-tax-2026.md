Pull up a lounger. Paperwork in Spain can give you a big headache, so I thought it was worth sitting down and looking at what’s actually changed this autumn, the bits that could leave you in the dark if you’re not paying attention.

I know a lot of you are somewhere in the middle of a TIE application, a renewal, or just trying to keep the non-resident tax side of things straight. A couple of changes are worth knowing about, especially if you’re in Alicante province.

**Automatic TIE appointments (pilot in Alicante)**

From early October 2026, the National Police expanded a pilot scheme that automatically assigns fingerprinting (toma de huellas) appointments for some people who have already received a favourable residence decision.

Alicante is now included, along with Valencia, Madrid, Barcelona, Málaga and Murcia.

Here’s how it works in practice:

* It currently applies to roughly 10% of favourable decisions issued after 8 October 2026.
* If you’re selected, you should be notified of the date, time and police station (usually by email or SMS).
* You don’t get to choose the slot.
* If nothing arrives within about 20 days of your decision, you still need to book the normal way through the cita previa system.

The system is still being tested, so most people will continue booking manually for now. The extraordinary regularisation window closed back in June, but the backlog from it is still affecting waiting times in many offices. Extra staff and some afternoon/Saturday openings are helping in places, but slots can still disappear quickly.

Practical bit: Keep an eye on your email and the official portal. Don’t assume silence means you’re in the automatic system.

**Modelo 210 — changes for 2026 income**

There are also new filing windows for non-resident tax (Modelo 210) on income from 2026 onwards. These come from Order HAC/623/2026.

* Imputed income (property you use yourself and don’t rent out): for 2026 income, you file between 1 April and 31 December 2027.
  (The window for 2025 imputed income is unchanged and still runs until 31 December 2026.)
* Rental income with tax to pay: whether you file it grouped for the year or separately, the deadline moves to 1–20 April 2027.

From 1 January 2027, the form itself will ask for a bit more detail (number of days the property was available or rented, your ownership share, and a clearer breakdown of expenses if you’re claiming them).

The tax rates stay the same: 19% if you’re resident in an EU/EEA country with information exchange, and 24% for everyone else (including the UK).

Practical bit: If you’re a non-resident property owner, note the later opening date for 2026 imputed income and the April 2027 window for rental income. It’s easy to miss when the calendar shifts.

That’s the useful update from this side of the paperwork mountain. Nothing revolutionary, but both changes are the kind of thing that can trip people up if they’re working off last year’s dates or assumptions.
