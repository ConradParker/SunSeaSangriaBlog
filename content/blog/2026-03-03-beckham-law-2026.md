The **Beckham Law**, formally Spain's special tax regime for impatriates (régimen especial de impatriados), remains a powerful national incentive in 2026 for attracting skilled professionals, entrepreneurs, remote workers, and investors to relocate to the country—including the **Comunidad Valenciana**. Qualifying individuals who move to Spain for eligible work reasons can opt to be taxed as non-residents for income tax purposes, paying a flat **24%** rate on Spanish-sourced earnings up to **€600,000** per year (rising to **47%** on amounts above that), while largely exempting foreign-sourced income—such as overseas investments, dividends, or rental yields—from Spanish taxation. This offers substantial savings compared to standard progressive rates, which can exceed **50%** (including regional surcharges) in areas like Valencia when taxing worldwide income. The regime applies for six years: the year residency begins plus the next five. Key eligibility includes not having been a Spanish tax resident in the prior **five** years (a rule relaxed since 2023 reforms), relocating specifically for qualifying employment, a directorship, innovative entrepreneurship, or international remote work (often tied to the digital nomad visa), and formally applying to the tax authorities within six months of starting work or social security registration. This makes it especially attractive for high-earning expats choosing vibrant regions like Valencia, Alicante, or Castellón, where it can dramatically lower effective tax burdens.
