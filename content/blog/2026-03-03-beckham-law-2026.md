Ok, Lets get to the bottom of the **Beckham Law**, formally Spain's special tax regime for expatriates (régimen especial de impatriados), which remains a powerful national incentive in 2026 for attracting skilled professionals, entrepreneurs, remote workers, and investors to relocate to the country, including the **Comunidad Valenciana**. Qualifying individuals who move to Spain for eligible work reasons can opt to be taxed as non-residents for income tax purposes, paying a flat **24%** rate on Spanish-sourced earnings up to **€600,000** per year (rising to **47%** on amounts above that), while largely exempting foreign-sourced income—such as overseas investments, dividends, or rental yields—from Spanish taxation. This offers substantial savings compared to standard progressive rates, which can exceed **50%** (including regional surcharges) in areas like Valencia when taxing worldwide income. The regime applies for six years: the year residency begins plus the next five. Key eligibility includes not having been a Spanish tax resident in the prior **five** years (a rule relaxed since 2023 reforms), relocating specifically for qualifying employment, a directorship, innovative entrepreneurship, or international remote work (often tied to the digital nomad visa), and formally applying to the tax authorities within six months of starting work or social security registration. This makes it especially attractive for high-earning expats choosing vibrant regions like Valencia, Alicante, or Castellón, where it can dramatically lower effective tax burdens.

Applying for the **Beckham Law** (the special tax regime for expatriates under Article 93 of Spain's Personal Income Tax Law) is a national process handled by the Spanish Tax Agency (**Agencia Tributaria** or AEAT), so the steps are identical whether you settle in the **Comunitat Valenciana** (Valencia region) or elsewhere in Spain. The regime is not managed or altered at the regional level.

To apply successfully, follow these key steps in order:

1. **Secure your move and initial registrations** — Relocate to Spain for a qualifying reason (such as an employment contract with a Spanish company, a directorship role, starting an innovative business, or international remote work often linked to the digital nomad visa). Obtain your **NIE** (Número de Identificación de Extranjero) if you don't already have one, register as a fiscal resident if required (commonly via Modelo 030 or 036/037 for census purposes), and crucially, register with Spanish **Social Security** (Seguridad Social) — this date typically starts the six-month countdown.
2. **Prepare and submit Modelo 149** — This is the official form to opt into the regime (or later renounce or be excluded from it). You must file it **electronically** through the AEAT's online portal (sede.agenciatributaria.gob.es), ideally with Cl@ve, an electronic certificate, or assistance from a representative. The strict deadline is **six months** from the date you start work/activity as shown on your Social Security registration (or equivalent proof if registration isn't mandatory).
3. **Attach supporting documents** — Include evidence that you meet the rules, such as:

Your employment contract or proof of directorship/appointment.

Social Security registration certificate or start-of-activity document.

Passport copy and NIE.

Proof you weren't a Spanish tax resident in the previous **five** tax years (e.g., prior tax returns or residency certificates from other countries).

For entrepreneurs, in certain cases, additional reports (like ENISA approval for startups).

1. **Await approval and handle ongoing obligations** — The AEAT reviews and usually issues a certificate of acceptance (within weeks to a few months). Provide this to your employer so they withhold tax at the special **24%** flat rate on qualifying salary (up to €600,000). In future years, file your annual income tax return using the special **Modelo 151** (instead of the standard Modelo 100).

The process is the same across Spain, including the Comunitat Valenciana, with no extra regional paperwork needed. Missing the six-month window means you cannot apply for that tax year onward.
