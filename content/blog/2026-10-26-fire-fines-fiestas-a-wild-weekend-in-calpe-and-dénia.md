Hola Terrace Crew,

**Calpe** has been buzzing this weekend with the fiestas! While family time and a busy weekend in Benidorm kept me from joining the celebrations directly, we still caught the festive energy through the booming fireworks and packed car parks. The Costa Blanca never sleeps, so let’s see what else has been happening around the region this week.

The one that caught people off guard was the car that turned into a fireball on Carrer Calp in Dénia. Just after midnight on Saturday into Sunday (the clock-change night), a parked vehicle caught fire. The video shows the flames taking hold quickly, the alarm screaming, and the bombers arriving from the local station before it jumped to the car parked right behind. Contained to the one vehicle. The cause is still unknown. That street already has a history: an abandoned house burned there twice last year, so locals were not entirely surprised, but it still lit up the phones.

**Dénia’s** local police had a productive Saturday afternoon of their own. Their prevention unit ran a special road and street operation and immobilised nine vehicles. On the e-scooters (VMP) side alone, they found eleven unregistered, one on the pavement, one without a helmet and one without the mandatory insurance. Ordinary cars and motorbikes added a no-licence offence, a noise-level breach, an invalid permit, a seatbelt fine and three ITV problems. There was also a drugs seizure and a public-drinking ticket. If you ride a patinete around the centre, the message is fairly clear: register it, stay off the pavements, and keep the paperwork handy.

{{< image-caption src="/images/autumn-vibes.png" >}}Autumn on the Costa Blanca never stops the Fiestas{{< /image-caption >}}

**Calpe’s** Moros y Cristianos for the 50th anniversary (now Fiesta of National Tourist Interest) wound through the weekend. The big Saturday desfilada filled the streets, Sunday morning brought the desembarco and first battle on Arenal-Bol with the usual gunpowder and boats against the Peñón backdrop, and the evening “El Miracle” played out in the Plaza Mayor. Capitanes this year were José Luis Moreno (Berberiscos) and Juan Luis Quero (Marins Corsaris). The formal close is tonight, Monday 26th, with the mass of the dead at 19:30 in the parish church of Nuestra Señora de las Nieves. If you were hoping for a quiet weekend in town, you were in the wrong place; if you like the noise, you got the full version.

T**he Fira de Tots Sants** is still up at Torrecremada in Dénia until 1 November. Saturday morning was reserved exclusively for people from disability associations and centres so they could go round the rides without the usual crush. Regular hours are from 17:00 on weekdays and Saturdays, with Sunday mornings as well. Reduced-price days for kids continue (including Wednesday 28th, which is also one of the quieter, lower-music days aimed at children with autism). Worth knowing if you have little ones or just fancy dodgems and fried food this week.

**Xàbia and Dénia’s** Ojos Rojos photography festival opened over the weekend (runs to 29 November, Argentina as guest country). Exhibitions are already up in Xàbia at places including the Museu Soler Blasco and Ca Lambert; Dénia’s part starts on the 30th. One of the stronger shows is Daniel Sánchez Alonso’s black-and-white editorial portraits from the late nineties and early 2000s. Free entry, so an easy afternoon if the weather stays kind.

**Sun Sea Weather** 

Looking ahead at the week, enjoy today’s mild and dry spells while you can, because **the autumn weather is on the turn across the Costa Blanca North**. Following a brief Indian summer bounce, AEMET data indicates that a transition toward a wetter, cooler climate will pick up speed starting Wednesday, with heavy cloud cover and rain probabilities ramping up. By Thursday and Friday, daytime temperatures are expected to take a visible dip as localised showers and gusty winds move into the Marina Alta. If you are planning a trip up to the Torrecremada fair later in the week or just keeping up with your daily run, it's definitely time to dust off the light jackets and keep an eye on the afternoon skies.
