Hola Terrace Crew,

Weekends go by quicker than a Formula 1 car these days, don’t they? Did you catch the Las Vegas Grand Prix? Great racing and more overtakes than I’ve seen in years. Made for a decent Sunday on the sofa.

Anyway, back to our little corner of Spain. The thing that actually stuck with everyone last week was the heat that simply would not leave the nights. A warm west wind blew through the Marina Alta and left us with a second night of tropical temperatures, ok not like summer tropical but still warm for November, temperatures like 22.3 °C in Dénia, 21.8 °C in Calpe, and the same warm air sitting over the Verger and the Poblets. By eight in the morning some spots were already past 27 °C. Inland the gusts went over 100 km/h. Most of us just left the windows open and waited for the wind to do something useful. It didn’t.

**Dénia, Jávea, Calpe and Teulada have shed more than 2,000 tourist lets in a year.** The latest INE figures (May 2025 to May 2026) show the four towns together dropped 2,025 advertised holiday homes, a 17.7 % fall. Jávea took the biggest percentage hit at 23.3 %, Dénia lost the most in raw numbers (820), Teulada fell 16.7 %, and Calpe the least at 7.6 % (241 fewer, down from 3,184 to 2,943). Calpe still has the highest share of its whole housing stock listed as tourist, 11.25 %. The numbers only count what’s being advertised on the platforms. They don’t prove those homes have suddenly turned up as long-term rentals. Local chat reckons a fair few have just gone quiet or stayed off the books. Either way, the public supply has shrunk a lot.

**Dénia’s big northern plan is moving again.** The town has put the partial plan for the Playa-Torrecremada sector out to tender, 43.5 hectares that could hold up to 1,888 homes, around 500 of them protected, plus a new school, parking and green space, and the long-promised Ronda Nord link toward the port. The council has already turned down the Generalitat’s denser version that would have pushed heights toward ten storeys. They said they don’t want another Benidorm. Nothing is approved yet. Flood studies and sea-level impact work still have to be done, and the terrace chat is already about how many years this will actually take. It’s the biggest planning conversation in Dénia right now.

**Dénia’s cheese fair finally happened.** The sixth “Dénia ¡está como un queso!” at Els Magazinos ran yesterday after the original October date got washed out by DANA Adam. Local makers from Callosa and Alcoy, plus a couple from further afield and a French blue, tastings and a proper market. If you missed it, you missed the one that actually went ahead this time.

**Premis Ovidi packed Dénia’s Centre Social.** The big night for music in Valencian happened on the 21st. Full house, the usual awards, speeches and the odd emotional tribute. It’s one of those evenings that quietly shows the town still has a proper cultural calendar once the summer noise dies down.

Practical line: the wind is still knocking about (gusts in the 30–40 km/h range yesterday and today) and the N-332 feels more exposed when it does. If you’re parking under trees or near the port edges, give the loose branches a second look before you walk away from the car.
