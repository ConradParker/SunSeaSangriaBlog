The Beckham Law, formally known as Spain's special tax regime for expatriates, is a national fiscal incentive designed to attract skilled professionals, entrepreneurs, and remote workers to the country, including the Comunidad Valenciana. It allows qualifying individuals who relocate to Spain for work purposes to pay a flat 24% rate of personal income tax on their Spanish-sourced earnings up to €600,000 annually (with 47% applying above that threshold), rather than facing the higher progressive rates that can exceed 50% in regions like Valencia when combined with state and regional levies. Crucially, foreign-sourced income—such as overseas investments, dividends, or rentals—remains largely exempt from Spanish taxation during the regime's duration of six years (the year of arrival plus the following five). Eligibility typically requires not having been a Spanish tax resident in the previous five years, moving for a qualifying job, directorship, startup venture, or international remote work (often linked to the digital nomad visa), and submitting an application to the tax authorities within six months of starting employment. This makes it particularly appealing for high-earning expats settling in vibrant areas like Valencia, Alicante, or Castellón, where it can significantly reduce overall tax burdens compared to standard residency rules.
