Hola Terrace Crew! ☀️🍷

Pull up a lounger and grab something cold. A few of you have asked what’s going on with “all these new driving rules,” and the headlines have not helped. Some of it is already here. The big rewrite lands on **1 October 2026**. 

This is the version for people who actually drive the N-332, sit behind a peloton on the way to Jalón, and occasionally take a wrong turn into a city with cameras.

**The date that matters**

The government has rewritten parts of the General Traffic Regulations to protect what it now officially calls usuarios vulnerables: walkers, cyclists, e-scooter riders, and motorcyclists. Most of it starts on October 1. A couple of scooter-light and moto-helmet details wait until **October 2027**.

**The one that will catch most of us: overtaking bikes**

If you use the N-332, the climb inland, or the Sunday run towards Guadalest, you already know how many bikes we get.

From 1 October, passing a cyclist, or a group, a pedestrian or a scooter,  on an interurban road means all of this at once:

* Still leave **1.5 metres**.
* On a road with more than one lane your way, you **move fully into the next lane**. Hugging the line does not count.
* You **drop at least 20 km/h below the posted limit** before and during the pass. On a 90 road, that means 70 or less.

Get it wrong, and you’re in the region of **€200 and four to six points**, depending on how they class the manoeuvre. The speed drop is the bit nobody’s muscle memory has yet. Practise it now, while it’s still just good manners.

In town, if you’re behind a bike in the same lane, hold about **five metres**. No more riding their back wheel through Calpe old town.

**Traffic lights at crossings**

Smaller change, but it will still feel odd.

At crossings with their own pedestrian lights, a flashing amber for cars should not run at the same time as a green man. The amber comes after a proper red. In English: green man on, cars wait.

Town halls have to reprogram the lights that don’t already work that way. Until they do, look at the people, not only the lamp.

**E-scooters (those pesky patinetes)**

National rules at last, instead of every town inventing its own:

* Minimum age **15**. Under that, not on a public road.
* **Helmet on.** No excuse.
* Pavements are off limits, including the path down to the beach.
* Lights and a reflective vest at night or in poor visibility about time, in my view)
* **Insurance has been required since early 2026** (the register that makes it stick arrived at the end of January).

Daytime lights on scooters get a year’s grace, until October 2027. The “teen in sliders on the pavement” summer classic is about to become a **€200** chat with the local police.

**Bikes and motos**

Cyclists lose the old “it’s too hot / I’m an adult” helmet get-outs on interurban roads. Helmet on. Delivery riders need a helmet and high-vis the whole shift.

If you ride a moto or a moped:

* **Gloves** on interurban roads from October. The fancy “homologated” spec follows when the ministry publishes the order; until then, real gloves, not oven mitts.
* **Closed shoes** on every road. Flip-flops on a rental scooter down to La Fossa will not do.
* In a proper jam, bikes may use the hard shoulder **if it’s signed** and they keep the speed right down (around 30 km/h).

Stricter homologated moto helmets come in a year.

**Already in force, don’t wait for October.**

**The V16 beacon.** Since 1 January 2026, Spanish-registered cars, vans and the like should carry a DGT-approved **connected** V16 light for breakdowns. Triangles on their own no longer count. Keep the high-vis vest in the cabin, not the boot. The fine for not having the beacon is modest, about **€80**, half if you pay promptly. Ignore the €30,000 rumours.

If you’re here on foreign plates for more than a flying visit, take a connected V16 anyway. Spanish rules are written for Spanish cars; triangles will not impress anyone on the AP-7.

**Zonas de Bajas Emisiones (ZBE).** Not invented this year, but more cameras are actually fining now. Standard fine is **€200** (€100 if you pay promptly), no points. Every city over 50,000 is supposed to have one, and each town writes its own small print. Check before you drive into Alicante or Valencia. Foreign plates often need registering first. Our stretch of coast is still milder than Madrid, but an old diesel and a shrug is not a plan.

**New cars** registered from **7 July 2026** come with a **socket** for an alcohol interlock. That’s a connector, not a breathalyser on the dashboard. Courts order the actual device. The car you’ve had for years is unaffected.

**What is not changing (despite the forwards)**

* The drink-drive limit is still **0.5 g/l** generally, **0.3** for new drivers and professionals. Politicians knocked back the 0.2 idea earlier this year.
* The theory test on 1 October stays the same: **30 multiple-choice questions**. No essays. No risk videos. DGT has said so out loud.
* Code 78 (automatic-only licence) is not disappearing next month. The EU path for adding manuals after a short course exists; Spain has until later in the decade to bring that in.

**Lorries and holiday weekends**

The usual DGT truck bans on certain motorways over summer weekends and fiesta dates are still a thing. They don’t target your Corsa. They do change how the AP-7 feels. Check dgt.es before the next operación retorno.

**What I’d actually do this week**

If you drive: start leaving that full lane and dropping speed around bikes now.



If there’s a scooter in the house: rider 15+, proper helmet, off the pavement, insurance sorted.



If you ride a moto: gloves in the top box, real shoes on both of you.



If you still only have triangles: buy a connected V16.



If you visit city centres: look up that town’s ZBE before you go.

None of this is meant to spoil the run to Dénia market. It’s just the moment where “we’ve always done it this way” meets a date on the calendar.

This is a plain-English read of the DGT / BOE changes, not legal advice. Town halls still set some of the local detail: which lights get reprogrammed first, where scooters may go, how fierce the ZBE cameras are.
