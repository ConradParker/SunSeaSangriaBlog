**Happy Halloween to all the witches, ghosts, and ghouls out there,**

Pull up a coffin (the loungers are looking a bit too scary this week). The tourists have mostly packed up, the light’s gone soft and golden, and the Nit de les Ànimes has properly rolled into the Northern Costa Blanca. Forget the plastic pumpkins, because this stretch from Dénia to Calpe has its own ghosts, bubotas, and slightly ridiculous local legends that have been hanging around far longer than any Halloween shop. Grab something cold (and maybe keep one eye on your glass) and let’s have a proper terrace chat about the spooky bits the brochures never mention.

**A few ghost stories from round here**

These are the ones people still half-mention when the sangria’s flowing and the evening’s gone quiet. Most have some grounding in local history or folklore; a couple are more “people say…” than documented tales.

**The sea cave that doesn’t like company**


Between Jávea and Dénia is **Cova Tallada** - a partly natural, partly hand-cut sea cave that was used as a quarry for the local tosca stone (you can still see the cut marks). Muslim-period remains have turned up inside, King Philip III is recorded as visiting in 1599, and later stories claim it was used as a supply point for submarines in the Second World War. It’s stunning in daylight and properly atmospheric once the light drops. The place has a reputation for being tricky (currents, slippery rock, sudden sea changes), and some visitors talk about the odd, unsettling feeling in the darker chambers. Take a torch, check the access rules, and don’t go alone.


More practical info:[ javea.com/la-cova-tallada](https://en.javea.com/la-cova-tallada/) or the local access sites.

**The English Cemetery in Dénia**

Tucked up above Marineta Cassiana is the little private cemetery opened in 1856 for non-Catholic Brits who couldn’t be buried in the municipal ground. About 14 people were interred there until 1918; the bodies were later repatriated, and the place has been left to the undergrowth. A local legend (muddled with an earlier 1799 shipwreck that has nothing to do with this cemetery) claims you can hear drunken sailors singing on full-moon nights. Local historians are clear the sailors weren’t buried here, so the singing is almost certainly wind and waves. Still a quiet spot with a good view and a strong “was that something or just the sea?” atmosphere.



Background:[ denia.com/cementerio-de-los-ingleses](https://en.denia.com/cementerio-de-los-ingleses/).

**The monk who never left Jávea**


In the old-town fortress-church of **Sant Bartomeu** (begun in the early 14th century and later enlarged as a defensive building), there is a long-running local story of a 14th-century monk who died during a plague and never quite checked out. People talk about cold spots, moving shadows, and candles that light themselves. The building already has that heavy historical feel; the legend simply adds a shiver. Plague outbreaks did hit the area in the late medieval period, which is why the story sticks.



Church details:[ javea.com/iglesia-de-san-bartolome](https://en.javea.com/iglesia-de-san-bartolome/).

**Our own local monsters**


Before the imported Halloween stuff arrived, the Marina Alta had its own cast of characters. **Bubotas** (also called buberotas, bumberotes, etc., depending on the village) are mischievous spirits used to scare children; traditionally, people hollowed a pumpkin, put a candle inside, draped a sheet over it and chased the kids. **Joanaina** around Teulada-Moraira appears on the night of Sant Joan near the Font de la Jana; she clings to your back, and you must reach the church of Santa Caterina without looking back if you want a wish. **Mare dels Peixos**, the sea-serpent of the Granadella, used to bother Jávea fishermen (she appears in an Enric Valor *rondalla*). Dénia’s current celebrations lean into these on purpose.


Folklore round-up:[ lamarinaalta.com piece on local monsters](https://lamarinaalta.com/antes-de-zombis-vampiros-y-hombres-lobo-los-monstruos-que-aterrorizaron-a-la-marina-alta/).

**Voices at the Peñón and Altea’s white lady**



Around Calpe’s Peñón de Ifach, people have long reported strange echoes and calls on stormy nights (usually blamed on wind and waves, until you hear them). The rock has several older legends (Isabel the heroine, sirens, etc.). In Altea’s old town there’s the classic woman in white who appears in the narrow streets and vanishes if you get too close, tied to a tragic widow’s story. Classic Mediterranean ghost territory.

**This weekend’s practical notes (Halloween / Nit de les Ànimes / Tots Sants)**

**Dénia – Nit de les Ànimes** (“Que tremole Halloween!”)



Running through early November, with the big day on **Saturday 31 October**. Valencian monster parade from Calle La Vía around 17:30, family activities earlier, more adult vibe later (dancing, disco).


Official:[ Ajuntament de Dénia notice](https://www.denia.es/va/denia/actualitat/noticia.aspx?id=7813) and coverage on[ denia.com](https://www.denia.com/denia-planta-cara-a-halloween-con-sus-propios-monstruos-desfiles-baile-pero-sin-pasaje-ni-tunel-del-terror/) /[ marinaalta.es](https://marinaalta.es/2026/10/denia-celebra-la-nit-de-les-animes-2026-con-desfiles-de-monstruos-valencianos-musica-y-actividades-para-halloween/).

**Jávea – BubotaFest**



**Saturday 31 October**. Terrifying street parade (with BATUCAM) leaving Plaza Marina Alta at 18:00, children’s show, music, mobile disco later in Plaça de la Constitució.



Details:[ explorejavea.com](https://explorejavea.com/events/halloween-bubotafest-2026-10681/) or @fogueresxabia.

**Cocentaina – Fira de Tots Sants (680th edition)**



Just the other side of the hills. Opens commercially Thursday 29 evening; main days **30 October–1 November**. Medieval-market energy, food, crafts, livestock, and far fewer plastic spiderwebs. Huge traditional fair.



Info: local coverage on[ levante-emv.com](https://www.levante-emv.com/comunitat-valenciana/2026/10/26/cocentaina-vuelve-convertirse-gran-escaparate-bc-135685582.html) and the town’s usual channels.

It’s still warm enough for the terrace after dark. Perfect weather for a slightly spooky evening wander followed by something cold.

Whether you believe any of it or you just like a good story with a sea view, the Northern Costa Blanca does Halloween its own way — local monsters, old walls, and the odd unexplained draft that might just be the evening breeze.

What’s the spookiest (or funniest) thing you’ve heard around here? Drop it in the comments — the terrace crew is listening.
