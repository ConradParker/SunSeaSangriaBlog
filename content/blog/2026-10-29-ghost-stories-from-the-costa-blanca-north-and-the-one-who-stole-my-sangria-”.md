**Hola Terrace Crew ☀️🍷**

Right then. The big summer rush has properly packed up, the light’s gone all soft and golden, and Halloween (or the local *Nit de les Ànimes*) has rolled into our little stretch of the Northern Costa Blanca. Forget the plastic pumpkins for five minutes — this bit from Dénia through Jávea and Calpe has its own ghosts, bubotas, and slightly ridiculous old stories. Pull up a lounger, grab something cold, and let’s have a proper terrace chat about the spooky bits the glossy brochures never mention.

**A few ghost stories from round here**

These are the ones people still half-mention when the sangria’s flowing and the evening’s gone quiet:

**The sea cave that doesn’t like company**

Between Jávea and Dénia there’s Cova Tallada — a partly underwater cave that was cut by hand for stone and later used by smugglers. The story is that some of them never came back out. Divers have said they’ve felt something tug at them in the dark or heard voices when no one else was around. It’s stunning in the daylight. Once the light drops it gets properly atmospheric. Take a torch.

**The English Cemetery in Dénia**

Tucked up above Marineta Cassiana is the little cemetery built in the 1850s for non-Catholic Brits. The old tale (slightly tangled with an earlier shipwreck) claims that on full-moon nights you can hear drunken sailors singing. It might just be the wind and the waves. Or it might not. Either way it’s a quiet spot with a cracking view — perfect for a “was that singing or just the sea?” moment.

**The monk who never left Jávea**

In the old-town church of Sant Bartomeu there’s a long-running story about a 14th-century monk who died during a plague and never quite checked out. People talk about cold spots, moving shadows, and candles that light themselves. The building already has that heavy, historical feel. The legend just adds an extra little shiver.

**Our own local monsters**

Before the imported Halloween stuff arrived, the Marina Alta had its own cast of characters: bubotas (mischievous spirits that chase kids), the Joanaina around Teulada-Moraira, and the Mare dels Peixos that used to bother Jávea fishermen near the Granadella. Dénia’s current celebrations lean into these on purpose — “Que tremole Halloween!” — with monster parades and local folklore instead of just the usual zombies.

**Voices at the Peñón and Altea’s white lady**

Around Calpe’s rock, people have reported calls for help echoing off the cliffs on stormy nights (usually blamed on wind and waves… until you hear it). In Altea’s old town there’s the classic woman in white who appears in the narrow streets and vanishes if you get too close — tied to a tragic widow’s story. Classic Mediterranean ghost territory.

**This weekend’s practical terrace notes**

Dénia’s Nit de les Ànimes is running now through early November, with the big day on the 31st: Valencian monster parade from Calle La Vía around 17:30, family stuff earlier in the day, and a more adult vibe later. Jávea has its own BubotaFest flavour. If you fancy a day trip inland, Cocentaina’s massive Fira de Tots Sants (its 680th edition) kicks off just the other side of the hills with medieval-market energy, food, and far fewer plastic spiderwebs.

It’s still warm enough for the terrace after dark. The heat has eased, the crowds have thinned, and the light is gorgeous. Perfect weather for a slightly spooky evening wander followed by something cold.

Whether you believe any of it or you just like a good story with a sea view, the Northern Costa Blanca does Halloween its own way — local monsters, old walls, and the odd unexplained draft that might just be the evening breeze.
