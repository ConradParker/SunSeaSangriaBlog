 **Pull up a lounger… it’s midsummer and the Costa Blanca North is busy and hot.**

Hola Terrace Crew!

July is in full swing and August is just days away. I’ve been following Mr Weatherman Europe on YouTube (one of the best channels for European weather), and his outlook for August is another long heatwave. Natural parks have already closed in July because of the extreme temperatures. Towns are packed with tourists, parking is pure luck, and the beaches are overflowing.

So here’s my no-fluff mid-summer list — the things that actually turn these weeks from a sauna with better views into the dream.

**1. Hit the beach before 10am (or after 6pm)**

Setting out early and finding quieter calas that still look magical when the light is soft and the sand isn’t frying eggs. Midday? Leave it to the brave (or the people who forgot sunscreen).

**2. Find a proper shaded terrace with a breeze**

Not every “sea view” terrace is equal. Look for the ones that catch the afternoon *terral* or have proper trees. My current favourites change with the wind, but a cold tinto de verano never does.

**3. Make (or order) real Spanish sangria the proper way**

Skip the neon tourist jugs. Young Tempranillo or Garnacha, plenty of fresh orange and lemon, a splash of brandy or Cointreau, sugar to taste, and leave it in the fridge for hours. Top with a little sparkling water only when you pour. Instant terrace therapy.

**4. Walk the Benissa Eco Path or a short coastal stretch at golden hour**

Flat, beautiful, and the sea breeze actually works. Perfect when the mountains are closed or simply too hot.

**5. Do your shopping and errands early**

Markets, post office, bank, supermarket — everything is easier (and cooler) before 11am. Afternoon siesta is not laziness; it’s strategy.

**6. Try a midweek cala that the day-trippers miss**

Cala de Morelló, the little spots around Cap Negret, or the quieter ends of the coast near Benissa and Altea. Fewer people, clearer water, and you can still find a rock to sit on.

**7. Keep a “heatwave kit” by the door**

Big water bottle, light scarf/hat, reef-safe sunscreen, and a small towel. You’ll thank yourself when you spontaneously decide the sea looks too good to ignore.

**8. Support the local evening markets and summer events**

Once the sun drops, the towns come alive again. Music in the plazas, open-air concerts, and the first stirrings of the bigger fiestas (Calpe’s Virgen de las Nieves is just around the corner).

**9. Have a backup indoor or high-up plan**

Museum, air-conditioned café, a friend’s pool, or simply the coolest room in the house with the shutters closed. Some days the smartest move is not fighting the heat.

**10. Remember why you (or we) are here**

The light at 8pm. The sound of the cicadas. The first proper cold drink of the evening. The fact that even on the hottest days, the Mediterranean is still right there.




That’s my midsummer list from the terrace this week.

What would you add? Favourite early-morning beach? Best shaded bar? Secret cala? Drop it in the comments — the Terrace Crew always knows the good stuff.

Stay cool (or at least stylishly warm),
