¡Hola from the Red Tape & Tears terrace,

Spain’s paperwork never stands still, and 2026 has already thrown a few proper curveballs. Here’s a simple round-up of the changes that actually affect most of us — without the legal waffle.

**1. Golden Visa is gone**

The property-based Golden Visa was abolished for new applications in April 2025.

If you already have one, you can still renew it, but no new ones are being issued. The investment route is closed.

**2. New Foreigners Regulation is fully in force**

The big new rulebook (RD 1155/2024) has been in force since May 2025. The main practical effects are:

* Clearer (and in some cases shorter) routes for *arraigo* (rooting)
* Changes to family reunification rules
* Updated processes for switching between different types of residency

**3. Big temporary regularisation programme (now closed)**

Earlier this year, there was an extraordinary regularisation window (open until 30 June 2026) for people who were already in Spain before 1 January 2026. That window has now closed.

**4. TIE cards and processing**

TIE (residence card) delays are still a regular headache in Alicante province. The process itself hasn’t dramatically changed, but appointment availability and waiting times continue to frustrate a lot of people.

**5. What hasn’t changed (but still catches people out)**

* You still need a **NIE** for almost everything financial or official.
* **Empadronamiento** (padrón) is still essential for healthcare, schools and many local procedures.
* The 183-day tax residency rule is still very much alive

**Bottom line for 2026**

The big investment visa route has gone, the general residency framework has been updated, and a one-off regularisation window has already closed. For most British and other non-EU residents already here, the day-to-day paperwork (NIE, TIE renewals, padrón, healthcare) continues much as before — just with the usual Spanish timing.
