Pull up a lounger (and maybe a light jumper). The Christmas lights are starting to appear, the first markets are opening, and the Costa Blanca North is doing what it does best, mixing local traditions with that easy seaside feel.

I’ve pulled together the confirmed dates we know so far for the main markets, special nights and shows. Programmes are still being finalised in a few towns,** *so always double-check the official tourist office or ayuntamiento page before you set off*.** Things can shift by a day or two.

**Early December markets (the busy weekend)**

The long bank holiday around the 6th is when a lot of the North lights up at once.

•  Dénia — Mercat de Nadal on Carrer La Via, 4–8 December. Local traders and craftspeople, decorations, gifts, workshops, storytellers and a visit from Santa. Easy to combine with a wander around the old town.

•  Jávea / Xàbia — Arts and crafts fair on the harbour promenade (Duanes de la Mar), 4–6 December. Unique handmade pieces with a sea view. Friday 5–8 pm, Saturday 11 am–8 pm, Sunday 11 am–7 pm.

•  Altea — Handcrafted nativity scene (every figure made by a different artisan) plus crafts and evening fire shows, 4–6 December. Avenida de la Comunitat Valenciana. Friday 6–9 pm, Saturday 11 am–2 pm and 5–9 pm, Sunday from 11 am.

•  Pedreguer (just inland) — Over 60 craftspeople at the lovely Jardín de l’Albarda, 28–29 November. Adults €9, children 7–13 €5, under 7s free. Saturday 10–14:30, Sunday 10–14:00. Perfect if you fancy a garden stroll while you shop.

•  Confrides (Guadalest Valley) — Christmas market with a proper ice rink and Father Christmas’s house, 4–6 December. Great family day out in the hills.

**Later in December**

•  Teulada — Christmas market with traditional festive products, 19–20 December.

•  Polop & Finestrat — Markets in the historic centres, 11–13 December (Polop has Santa’s house and family activities).

•  Dénia’s Nit de la Llum — Saturday 12 December. Thousands of candles light up the old town and shops stay open late. Magical and very local.

•  Calpe — Lights switch-on Friday 27 November at 19:00 in Plaza de la Sort, with a pasacalle and a merienda in Plaza de la Villa. Christmas concert by the Unión Musical Calp Ifach on Saturday 19 December at 19:30 in the Casa de Cultura (free until full). Other concerts and shows at the Casa de Cultura through December — check**[ cultura.calp.es](cultura.calp.es)**.

**Shows and special nights**

•  Teulada-Moraira Auditorium: Nutcracker ballet (International Ballet Company) on Saturday 19 December at 18:00 — €32. Alabama Gospel Choir on Sunday 27 December at 18:00 — €38. Tickets via auditoriteuladamoraira.es.

•  Benidorm (short hop south): Plaza de la Navidad opens 28 November with lights, ice rink, attractions and market, running through to early January. Big family atmosphere. Chloe de la Rosa show on the switch-on evening.

•  Jávea: Hebrew nativity at Museo Soler Blasco from 11 December to 6 January, plus various band pasacalles and church concerts through December.

**Practical terrace notes**

Most markets are free to wander (the Pedreguer garden is the main paid entry). Parking gets busier on the 4–6 December weekend, so go earlier in the day or walk from a side street. Take a jacket; it cools quickly once the sun drops. Many events are outdoors, so a jumper or scarf is sensible even if the day has been mild.

The big Three Kings parades (Cabalgatas de Reyes) are still being confirmed in most towns; they usually happen on 5 January and arrive by sea in places like Moraira and Jávea. I’ll update once the full programmes land.

If you’re looking for a real English-style panto, keep an eye on Benidorm venues and local English theatre groups; they often pop up closer to the date. The Spanish side of Christmas here is more markets, belenes, concerts and that lovely candlelit night in Dénia.
