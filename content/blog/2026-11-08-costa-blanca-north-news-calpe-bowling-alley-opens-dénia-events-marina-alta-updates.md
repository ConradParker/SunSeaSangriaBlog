Hola Terrace Crew! ☀️🍷

Another Monday, doesn't the weekend fly by?

Pull up a lounger, grab your coffee (or whatever’s keeping the weekend at bay), and let’s catch up on what’s been happening along our stretch of coast from Alicante up through Altea, Calpe, Jávea, Dénia and the towns in between. This one’s a bit fuller, the news has been busy.

The weather’s doing its usual mild November thing,  soft light, a bit of cloud, nothing dramatic. Perfect for a terrace afternoon if the sun decides to show its face.

Calpe’s new bowling alley is open

Big news for anyone who’s been moaning about the lack of evening options once the summer crowds leave. Bowling Calpe (the new spot from Grupo La Sentadita) opened this past Friday and it’s geared up for the whole family.

Twelve lanes, a kids’ zone with a ball pit, arcade games, billiards, football tables, and an American-style restaurant with a decent wine and champagne list. They’re open every day from 10:00 right through to 1:30 in the morning. Games start from €6 on weekday mornings (rising later in the day and at weekends).

It’s next to Another World, so you can do VR and bowling in one hit if the kids have energy. Local bowlers are already happy,  people like Pablo Hermosilla (who competes for Los Halcones de Sagunto) can finally train in town instead of schlepping to Benidorm or Valencia. Most of the staff are local too, so it’s a fab win for Calpe.

Other Calpe bits

The old Club Social at La Manzanera is still in the news after the roof collapse. The town has an emergency contract in place to shore up what’s left and secure the perimeter. There’s still the long-running tug-of-war with the coastal authorities over its future, and people are sharing old photos of the place when it was the social hub for the whole La Manzanera complex. It hurts to see another piece of that 1970s dream looking rough.

Calpe is also switching nearly all the old-town street lights to LED (190 of the 200 lamps), which should save the town over €16,000 a year and cut a decent amount of CO₂. Beach cleaning has a bigger budget and more staff from the start of the month, and there’s a new round of free online Spanish courses for international residents, 450 places, sign up by 15 November. Camina walking groups are running Tuesdays and Thursdays from the Sol y Mar stairs if you fancy a sociable stroll. La Cubana are bringing their new show to town mid-month too.

Dénia

Dénia Stock filled Marqués de Campo over the weekend with 45 local shops and a good crowd — the weather helped and the discounts clearly worked. The Tapas Route is still running until 22 November if you fancy a wander. There’s also a cheese festival (Dénia ¡está como un queso!) coming up on the 22nd at Els Magazinos after the earlier rain delay — local and wider producers, tastings, the works.

A young Balearic band and choir put on a Mediterranean-themed concert on Saturday, and there’s a contemporary dance festival at the Teatre Auditori from 11–15 November. The housing conversation continues hard here — another 22 affordable rental homes under the Casa 47 scheme are coming available across the comarca (including spots in Dénia, Pedreguer and Beniarbeig). Rents are meant to stay under 30% of household income with long contracts. There’s a general housing strike and demonstration planned for 11 November — three pickets around town and a bigger comarcal march.

Jávea (Xàbia)

Jávea is opening applications from 12 November for the first 56 protected affordable homes in the Aires del Montgó I complex. The desalination plant is also due to return to municipal management (via Amjasa) from 15 November, after the full council approved the switch. A quick note from the weekend: the Xàbia Negra Express short-film contest turned the old town into a film set with teams racing to finish in 36 hours — record entries this year.

Benissa, Teulada-Moraira, Altea and further afield

Benissa has been spotlighting its furniture-making history — the local chronicler has a new book on the old factories and workshops that once dominated the town. Teulada-Moraira has Santa Cecilia music events coming mid-month (the patron saint of musicians), and Moraira has Ugly Day with the bikes and stunts on 14–15 November. Altea is also celebrating Santa Cecilia with concerts and activities from the 14th.

Benidorm’s patron saint festivities (Virgen del Sufragio and Sant Jaume) have been running 6–11 November with the usual mix of processions, music, fireworks and street events — the big float parade and fireworks are still to come. Pego is pushing for better health services (more staff, advanced life support, stronger paediatrics and mental health cover) after a meeting with the Denia health department. Ondara’s Protección Civil has added 12 new members and refreshed its kit. Orba has secured extra funding so the new IES Enric Valour project can go ahead at a higher total.

Quick practicals and other bits

• Free Spanish courses for internationals in Calpe — deadline 15 November.

• TRAM Line 9 (serving Calpe) is getting overnight work through November to cut noise and vibration.

• Calpe hosted the Viles en Flor awards recently and kept its three Flors d’Honor.

• The Marina Alta is one of the oldest comarcas on the Valencian coast — nearly half the population is over 65 in some of the smaller villages.

• Keep an eye on local notices for any DANA-related recovery updates or road works — the ground is still settling in places after the earlier rain, and emergency works are continuing on some riverbeds around Els Poblets and La Rectoria.

That’s the round-up from our little corner of the coast. The sun’s trying, the sea is still inviting, the new bowling alley is open for business, the fiestas are still going in places, and the terrace is ready whenever you are.

Until next Monday.
