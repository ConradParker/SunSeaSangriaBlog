Hola Terrace Crew! ☀️🍷

Pull up a lounger, pour something cold, and let’s talk about the moment this coast exhales.

You can feel it before the calendar even turns. The hire cars start disappearing from the N-332. The last of the inflatable flamingos get packed into boot space or left at the bins. Supermarkets have your favourite things on the shelves again, and somewhere between the final August storm and the first evening that doesn’t glue your shirt to your back, Costa Blanca North remembers who it is when it isn’t performing for August.

I’ve lived this swap more than twenty times now. September is still my favourite month here. Not because it’s perfect. Because it’s ours again, selfish, I know.

***The light changes first***

August light is fierce and slightly yellow with clouds of calima at times. September light is cleaner. Softer at the edges. The Peñón de Ifach stops looking like it’s on fire at 6 pm and starts looking like itself again with trees turning green after autumn rains.

Mornings are my favourite; you can walk La Fossa or Les Marines before nine without weaving through a forest of umbrellas. The water is still 25–26°C most years, so nobody is putting the swimsuits away. They’re just putting a T-shirt on afterwards. Progress.

***Parking becomes a rumour that turns out to be true***

This is the underrated miracle.

In Calpe you can actually find a space near the port without circling like a confused seagull. In Jávea old town, the one-way streets stop feeling like a video game. Altea’s cobbles are still steep (they always will be), but you can hear your own footsteps again instead of a convoy of rolling suitcases.

Don’t get me wrong, it’s still busy with tourists, but the daily grind eases. You stop doing that little victory fist-pump when you reverse into a gap. That alone is worth the month, especially in Calpe where parking is a nightmare.

***The beaches don’t empty. They just calm down.***

Anyone who tells you “the tourists all leave on 1 September” has never been to Arenal in Jávea on a sunny Saturday in mid-month.

What actually happens is more interesting. The package crowds thin. The families with school-age kids start heading home. What’s left is a mix of residents, remote workers who finally leave the villa, and the people who deliberately book September because they already know.

***Coves that felt impossible in August*** — Cala del Mallorquí, parts of Les Rotes, the quieter end of La Fustera — become walkable again. You still need water and decent shoes. You just don’t need a military strategy.

**Posidonia** still gets the odd anchor dropped on it, because some habits die hard. But the marine patrols have more chance of being heard when they’re not shouting over three languages and a portable speaker.

***Towns go back to Spanish time.***

This is the bit visitors miss, and residents quietly celebrate.

Kitchens that spent August turning tables at 7 pm remember that dinner starts later. Bars fill with people who actually live here. You start recognising faces again, the same couple walking the dogs at dusk in Moraira, the same stallholders at Jávea market who’ve been slicing jamón since before some of the new apartment blocks existed.

***Markets feel like markets***, not obstacle courses. You can stop at a cheese stall without someone photographing the cheese as if it might escape.

Altea old town at 8 pm in September is one of the prettiest hours on this coast. White walls, blue dome, a glass of something cold, and conversation instead of a soundtrack of “where’s the car parked?”

***The heat lets go. Reluctantly***

Let’s not pretend September is autumn. It isn’t.

You’ll still get days in the low thirties. You’ll still need the fan at night for the first week or two. But the sticky 39°C midnights of late August usually loosen their grip. Evenings become usable. Terraces become the point again, not just the place you hide from the afternoon.

This is when I start thinking about lovely walks. Not the “dash to the shop and back before you melt” walks. Actual ones. Montgó in the morning. The salt flats in Calpe when the light is low and the flamingos are doing their thing. The first cooler hike of the season feels like getting a part of yourself back.

***Fiestas don’t stop. They just change gear.***

August is fireworks and gridlock. September is still festive, just less frantic.

Jávea’s Mare de Déu de Loreto runs into early September with the port still busy, bous a la mar energy, and that last big firework splash over the bay. Then Altea’s patronales and Moros y Cristianos arrive later in the month — colour, gunpowder, processions, and the particular joy of watching a town dress up as itself.

Teulada-Moraira’s vendimia sits right in this window too. Grapes, new wine, and the feeling that the year is turning even if the sea hasn’t noticed yet.

***If you only come in August, you miss this layer. The coast isn’t winding down. It’s changing with time.***

What I actually do in September

Same as most years, with small variations:

•  Swim earlier, walk later.

•  Reclaim a couple of restaurants that were booked solid for eight weeks.

•  Buy too much fruit at the market because the late tomatoes and first grapes are ridiculous.

•  Sit on the terrace with Missy after dark and listen to the cicadas finally lower the volume. (They have been very loud this summer)

•  Remember that this is why we stayed.

The paperwork doesn’t disappear. The N-332 still has opinions. Someone will still wear flip-flops into a place that deserved better. But the whole coast feels like it has shrugged off a heavy rucksack.

***If you’re visiting in September***

Come. Honestly, it's great.

Book a table. Don’t assume everywhere is closed midweek — a lot of places stay open precisely because this is when the regulars return. Swim. Walk the old towns in actual clothes. Give the sea a bit of respect after the summer pounding it took. And if you see a resident staring at an empty parking space with mild disbelief, that’s not rudeness. That’s twenty years of August leaving the body.

If you already live here: this is your month. Use the mornings. Stay out later. Let the terrace earn its keep.

The tourists don’t all leave, of course. They just stop being the main character.

And that, Terrace Crew, is when Costa Blanca North starts sounding like itself again.

Now if you’ll excuse me, I’m going to enjoy a September evening that doesn’t require a cold shower first.

With sun, sea and a slightly quieter sangria, 

Joanne xx
