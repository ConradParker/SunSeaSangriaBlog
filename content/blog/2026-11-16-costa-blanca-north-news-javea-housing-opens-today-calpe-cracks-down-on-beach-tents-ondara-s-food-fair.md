Hola Terrace Crew! ☀️🍷

Happy Monday. Pull up a lounger, grab your morning coffee and let’s catch up on what’s been happening along our stretch of coast from Dénia down through Jávea, Calpe and the bits in between.

The weather’s doing its usual mild November thing, soft light, a bit of cloud, nothing dramatic. Around Dénia/Jávea/Calpe we’re looking at highs in the low-to-mid 20s and overnight lows in the mid-teens, no heavy rain on the cards and no alerts. Perfect for a terrace afternoon when the sun decides to show its face.

**Jávea’s protected housing finally opens for applications today.**

If you’ve been watching the Aires del Montgó I development in Jávea, today’s the day. The 56 protected homes (46 for sale, 10 for rent) on Poeta José Albi street were meant to open for registration last Thursday, but a technical glitch on the Plan Vive platform forced a short delay. It’s now live as of today (Monday 16th). Prices for the sale ones are in the €177k–€253k range, including VAT, garage, and storage, with handover expected in 2028. Worth a look if you’re on the hunt for something more affordable in the area — the housing pressure here is real, and these don’t come around every week.

**Calpe draws a line on those giant beach tents.**

Calpe’s new coastal ordinance is in public consultation after the full council gave it initial approval. The big talking point is the ban on the big carpas, windbreaks and camping-style tents that have been turning parts of the sand into little private chalets. From now on, it’s umbrellas only, plus small igloos or tents up to one square metre strictly for little kids. The same package also creates a second dog beach at Cala Manzanera (joining Cala les Urques) and tightens up the ecological mooring rules. It’s the usual “quality tourism” push, but plenty of locals will be glad to see the sprawling setups reined in. The text is open for comments for 30 days.

**Ondara’s Fira de Fires is mid-swing**

The historic fair is running 14–22 November, concentrated into two weekends this year. The first one (just gone) focused on “La Marina amb bon gust”, local producers from across the comarca (oils from Gata, wine from Pedreguer and Jávea, coques, honey, the lot) at El Prado, plus demos of traditional crafts, tastings and live music. This coming weekend (21–22) brings the medieval market, animal fair, trade stands and the classic car meet on Avenida Doctor Fleming on Sunday morning. The funfair is up until the 25th on Calle Nacions Sense Estat, with quieter hours for anyone who prefers less noise. Real local flavour if you fancy a wander inland.

**Other bits worth knowing**

* A luxury catamaran burned and sank at Dénia marina last Monday after witnesses reported a bang. Firefighters isolated it so it didn’t spread; no one was on board, and there were no injuries. Investigations ongoing.
* The N-332 between Santa Pola and La Marina is closed from today for nearly three weeks while a new bridge goes in. Official diversion via the CV-865 / CV-851 / CV-853. Relevant if you’re heading south to the airport or beyond.
* Jávea’s week-long padel tournament (Go With The Flow x Club de Tenis Jávea) kicks off today and runs through the 22nd — group stages, flexible timing so it fits around normal life.
* Housing remains the hot topic everywhere. Rents for a decent 80 m² place in Dénia, Jávea or Calpe can still swallow a big chunk of the minimum wage, which is why the protected schemes and the ongoing “zona tensionada” push matter so much.
