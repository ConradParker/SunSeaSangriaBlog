**Dénia 2026: Fiestas That Capture the True Spirit of the Northern Costa Blanca**

If the northern Costa Blanca has a beating heart, it's Dénia. This lively port town doesn't just host fiestas—it lives them. From the roaring flames of Fallas lighting up the night sky to the dramatic clashes of Moors and Christians echoing off the castle walls, and the heart-racing sight of bulls plunging into the sea during Bous a la Mar, Dénia turns every celebration into a full-sensory explosion of colour, sound, and community pride.

Rooted in a history of conquests (the town flipped between Moorish and Christian control for centuries), its biggest events feel raw and real—not staged for visitors. The fishing roots show in sea-bound processions, while the Valencian passion for fire and satire shines in bonfires and satirical ninots. In 2026, the streets will once again fill with families, filaes in elaborate costumes, kids waving flags, and the smell of gunpowder and fideuà wafting everywhere.

This is the place where traditions aren't preserved in a museum—they're lived loudly, year after year. Here's your go-to guide to Dénia's 2026 fiesta calendar (dates are set where confirmed; moveable or religious ones follow patterns—always verify on denia.com, femmiccdenia.com, or the town hall app as summer approaches).

* 
  **January 6: Arrival of the Three Kings (Cabalgata de Reyes Magos)**


  A magical evening kick-off: Kings arrive by sea at the port around 5–6 pm, then parade through town tossing sweets to excited kids. Floats, lights, music—pure childhood wonder.


  **February 6–14: Mig Any Moros i Cristians**


  The "halfway" party is to build excitement for August. Flag-raisings at the castle, captain presentations, parades, and a big official desfile on Feb 14 at noon. See the filaes' outfits up close without the full summer crowds—perfect teaser.


  **Late February/Early March: Carnaval**


  Pre-Lent fun with costume contests, street parades, music, and the symbolic burial of the sardine. Lively, colourful, and great for all ages.


  **March (building to 19th): Fallas**


  Dénia's take on Valencia's iconic festival. Ninots (giant, satirical figures) go up in early March, with plantà around March 15–16, daily mascletà fireworks, street parties, and the dramatic cremà burnings on the night of March 19. Smaller scale than big-city Fallas but incredibly atmospheric—vote on favourites at the Ninot exhibition earlier in the year.


  **Holy Week (Semana Santa, around early April)**


  Solemn processions with religious floats, candles, and music through the old town. Moving and beautiful, especially at night.


  **June 23–24: Nit de Sant Joan**


  Beach bonfires, jumping over flames for good luck, and fireworks reflecting on the Mediterranean. Summer officially starts with a bang.


  **July 6–14: Fiestas Mayores in Honour of the Santíssima Sang**


  Patron saint week packed with maritime parades, concerts, street feasts, and the legendary 

  **Bous a la Mar**

  —bulls run through packed streets and leap into the harbour (a thrilling, unique spectacle). Peak energy mid-week; religious acts on the main day. Family events mix with high-adrenaline moments.


  **August 13–16: Moros y Cristianos**


  The summer blockbuster! Recreations of historic battles: dramatic Moorish landings at the port, massive parades of filaes in stunning costumes, mock combats with muskets and drums, and celebrations around San Roque (Aug 16). Gunpowder clouds, cheering crowds, castle views—unmissable.


  **Other yearly highlights**


  Virgen del Carmen (mid-July): Fishermen's tribute with boats carrying flowers out to sea and candlelit floats.
  Smaller local days like San Vicente (April) or San Mateo (September).
  Food festivals and Christmas markets round out the year.



  **Quick visitor tips for Dénia fiestas 2026**


  - July and August get busy—book hotels or apartments months ahead.
  - Prime spots: Castle ramparts for fireworks and parades; port area for Bous a la Mar (stay behind barriers!).
  - Bring comfy shoes, earplugs for fireworks, and respect the space—don't block filaes or touch costumes.
  - Kid-friendly overall, but supervise near bulls or fires. Try local specialities like fideuà during street dinners.
  - Stay hydrated and enjoy responsibly—these are community events, so join in the spirit!
