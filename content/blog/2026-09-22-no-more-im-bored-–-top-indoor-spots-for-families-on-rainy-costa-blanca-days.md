Hola, Terrace Crew. ☀️🍷

Pull up a lounger. If you are reading this, one of two things has happened. Either the sky over the Peñón has gone grey, or the kids are looking at you and about to say those three words that end a holiday: “I’m bored.”

Rain on the Costa Blanca North is rarely the week-long British version. It is usually short, mild and slightly chaotic, fifteen to twenty degrees, a proper dump on the Montgó, a flooded dip on the way into Dénia, then a patch of blue that tricks you into leaving the umbrellas in the car. I wrote that last bit from experience. Not long ago we pointed the car at Dénia castle on my husband’s day off and came home looking like we had swum the harbour.

The good news is you do not have to sit in the apartment watching the same cartoon on someone’s phone. From Calpe, Jávea and Dénia you can fill a wet morning, a whole afternoon, or a full family day without anyone needing a cagoule. These are the indoor and almost-indoor spots I actually send people to, not a brochure list, the ones that work when the kids are already wriggly, and Dad is “just along for the ride.”

*Drive times below are from Calpe in normal traffic. Add ten minutes from Moraira or Benissa. Knock a few off if you are already in Jávea heading north.*

**First: check the sky, then pick a radius.**

Open[ AEMET](https://www.aemet.es/en/eltiempo/prediccion/municipios) before you start the engine. Rain here is patchy. The cloud can sit on the Montgó and leave Calpe almost dry, or the other way round. If the radar shows a short cell, you can still do a covered stroll in Altea old town or Moraira’s harbour and call it a win. If it is a proper grey day, pick one roof and stay under it. Mixing three towns in one wet afternoon is how you end up eating petrol-station sandwiches in a steamed-up car.

A useful split:

* **Under 15 minutes.** J-Park in Calpe, Another World VR in Calpe, a covered wander in Calpe old town or the port.
* **20–35 minutes.** Portal de la Marina in Ondara, DAR Family Club on the Jesús Pobre road, Cine Jayan in Jávea, Dénia’s Toy Museum, Airjump in Finestrat.
* **45–70 minutes.** Plaza Mar 2 and Kinépolis in Alicante, Valor chocolate in Villajoyosa, Cuevas de Canelobre in Busot, Street Jump in Elche. Worth it if you want a full day out, not a quick fix.

**Shopping centres - the rain-proof all-dayer**

I know, I can hear the men moaning already, but it's not why we booked the Costa Blanca. On a wet Tuesday in February, though, a building with toilets, a food court, a cinema, and free parking isn't “selling out.” It is survival.

***Portal de la Marina, Ondara — 25 to 30 minutes***

This is the one. Locals just call it La Marina. Forty thousand square metres, open roughly 10:00 to 22:00, free parking, and it is pet-friendly if the dog is part of the holiday committee. You can genuinely lose a whole day here without anyone noticing the weather.

What is actually useful for families:

* **Shops.** Zara, Massimo Dutti, Pull&Bear, Bershka, Lefties, H&M, C&A, plus the usual sports, home and kids’ bits. There is an IKEA presence in the centre now, which is either a blessing or a two-hour detour depending on who you married.
* **Food.** Carrefour for groceries and the emergency biscuit run. Ten-plus places to eat, from 100 Montaditos and quick bites to sit-down. Nobody has to agree.
* **Cinema.** Cines IMF / Cines Mediterráneo upstairs,  eight screens, Dolby, 3D in some rooms. Monday and Wednesday are spectator days with cheaper tickets. Always check which films are VO or VOSE if you want English audio.
* **Kids.** Family zones and seasonal events; they ran a whole LEGO takeover in summer 2026. Toilets, a nursing room, Wi-Fi, Amazon lockers if you are the sort of person who has a parcel following you around Spain.

Centre site:[ portaldelamarina.org](https://www.portaldelamarina.org/). Check the cinema page before you promise a particular film.

***Plaza Mar 2, Alicante - 45 to 50 minutes***

Further, bigger, and worth it if you already need Alicante or you have older kids who want a better multiplex. Over 120 shops, a large hypermarket, a food court that can feed a mixed-age group without a committee meeting, and Kinépolis with 16 screens and around 2,800 seats.

Kinépolis is the reason to drive. Dolby, big screens, regular VOSE showings, cheaper tickets on Wednesdays, and they have been adding extra formats. If the rain eases, Santa Bárbara Castle is right there, free to walk, a couple of euros for the lift through the rock, and it will finish off whatever energy the food court didn't.

I wrote that Alicante-to-Dénia stretch up properly in my[ family road-trip guide](https://sunseasangria.com/blog/2026-08-25-costa-blanca-road-trip-alicante-to-denia-family-guide-2026/) if you want to turn the wet day into a two-stop outing.

Cinema:[ Kinépolis Plaza Mar 2](https://kinepolis.es/cines/kinepolis-alicante-plaza-mar-2/informacion/).

**Soft play and trampolines - kid heaven, parent coffee**

***DAR Family Club, Jesús Pobre road (Jávea) - about 20 to 25 minutes (Currently closed for renovations)***

If I could add only one place most “rainy day in Calpe” lists miss, it would be DAR. It sits on the road to Jesús Pobre, so it is handy from Jávea, Dénia, Gata and Pedreguer, and it is the indoor play area local families actually use rather than the one tourists find on a map and hope is open.

Soft play, trampolines, climbing bits, a café where you can sit down like a human, birthday parties, camps in the school holidays. They publish two simple price bands, a cheaper toddler slot and a meal-snack-and-drink option for older children,  but private parties swallow the space, so check before you drive. Open long hours most days, free parking, and it feels like someone’s house in the best way.

Check first:[ darcentre.com](https://www.darcentre.com/) or WhatsApp them. If a private party is on, the public soft play will be shut, and you will be the person doing a U-turn in the rain.

***J-Park - Calpe***

Avenida del Norte 35, teléfono 622 332 923. Soft play, ball pit, slides, a café, and they do birthdays with the full Spanish extras — characters, music, the lot. Super convenient if you are staying in[ Calpe](https://sunseasangria.com/blog/2026-06-16-where-to-stay-in-calpe-best-areas-for-expats-visitors-2026/) and the little ones just need to bounce for an hour.

Honest bit: hours here move around parties. It is still listed and reviewed, but I would not drive a car-load of children across town without a WhatsApp or online check. Typical public hours when they are open for drop-in are late afternoon into early evening, around 17:00–21:00. Book if you want the whole place.

***Airjump, Finestrat - 25 to 35 minutes towards Benidorm***

This is the energy dump. Indoor, air-conditioned, trampolines, parkour, foam pits, a climbing wall, a mechanical bull that will either delight or humiliate the adults, and a smaller zone for the under-sevens. Socks with grip are compulsory; they sell them if you forget.

Recent hours have been running roughly Monday to Friday 17:00–21:15 and weekends from noon. That midweek late opening is useful: beach in the morning if it is only grey, jump after lunch if it turns wet. Address is Avenida Rafael Alberti 8, Finestrat. Phones: 659 942 204 and 699 097 550.

Site:[ airjumpspain.es](https://www.airjumpspain.es/airjump-finestrat).

***Street Jump, Elche - a fun day trip***

Only go if you want the big one. More than 4,000 square metres, split by age: Adventure Park for roughly one to nine, Trampoline Park from about ten or 1.30 m tall. Slides, foam pit, basketball jump, climbing, inflatables depending on what is open that season. They sell their own jump socks, and sessions on the trampoline side start on the hour; arriving at ten past is how you lose fifty minutes of what you paid for.

Hours change with the season, and Sundays are the ones that catch people out. It is an industrial-estate location on Carrer Sabadell with easy parking. After the jumps, Elche itself is worth an hour. The palm groves are a World Heritage site and a lot of the old town is walkable even in a drizzle.

Check times and which zone is open:[ streetjump.es](https://streetjump.es/).

**Bowling, VR and laser -  the “Dad can join in” list**

***Another World VR, Calpe -  in town***

This is the surprise hit with mixed-age families. Free-roam virtual reality in a couple of hundred square metres opposite the bus station, next to Victoria Cars, on Avenida de la Generalitat Valenciana 1. You are not sitting in a chair with a headset. You are walking around an arena shooting things, escaping rooms or dancing badly, depending on the game.

Good from about eight or nine upwards. They also run family and birthday packs. Book. Walk-ins on a wet Saturday will be told the next slot is Tuesday. Recent pattern: closed Mondays, Tuesday to Friday from mid-afternoon; weekends from noon. From about €15 for 30 minutes or €24 for an hour midweek, more at weekends. Phone 672 453 079.

Book:[ calpe.another-world.com](https://calpe.another-world.com/).

**Ozone Bowling, Benidorm — 25 to 35 minutes**

Ten lanes on Avenida del Mediterráneo 22, a short hop from Levante. Simulators, arcade machines, Nicky’s snack bar. This is the original Ozone bowling in Spain more or less started on this street in 1974, and it still does the simple family job.

Typical hours: 11:00 until midnight Sunday to Thursday, until 01:00 Friday, Saturday and holiday eves. Last published prices were about €6 a game midweek and €7 at weekends, plus €1 for shoes on the first game. They do not reserve lanes, so a wet Saturday in half-term means go early or go late. Phone 965 854 187.

Laser tag has lived on this same stretch under a few names over the years. Formats and operators change, so treat “laser tag next to the bowling” as something to confirm on the day rather than a promise.

Ozone Benidorm:[ ozonebowling.com](https://www.ozonebowling.com/en/benidorm/).

**Museums, chocolate and caves -  dry, and not only for a rainy day**

***Museo del Juguete, Dénia - 30 to 35 minutes***

Free, compact, and in the old Dénia–Carcaixent station on Calle Calderón. The ground floor is the L’Estació art space; upstairs is a century of Dénia toys — wooden kitchens, tin cars, pedal planes, the industry that used to come out of this town before the tourism machine arrived. Kids who will not look at a “museum” will look at this. Adults go quiet in the good way.

Open most days with a lunch break. Closed 25 December, 1 January, 19 March and 1 May. Hours shift with the season. There is a lift to the first floor. Pair it with a covered lunch around the port, and you have a civilised wet morning that does not involve anyone bouncing off a wall.

Details:[ Dénia Toy Museum](https://denia.net/que-visitar/museo-del-juguete-y-centro-de-arte-lestacio/).

***Valor Chocolate Museum, Villajoyosa — 35 to 45 minutes***

The one that smells better than it has any right to. Valor has been making chocolate here since 1881. The visit is a museum, a short film, a look at the factory and a tasting,  about an hour to an hour and a half. Entry is free. That is not a typo. You do have to take a timed slot because they cap groups at fifty.

English tours are usually 10:00 and 16:00 Monday to Friday, and 10:00 on Saturday. Saturday afternoons, Sundays and holidays, the museum is closed; the shop keeps its own shorter Saturday hours. Book on the Valor site. They also shut for staff holidays in late July / early August and around Christmas, so check before you promise it as the day’s plan. Villajoyosa’s painted houses are a five-minute add-on if the rain thins.

Book the slot:[ valor.es](https://www.valor.es/).

**Cuevas de Canelobre, Busot — 40 to 50 minutes**

A cave is indoor by definition, and this one is the closest thing Alicante province has to a cathedral underground, a vault around seventy metres high. Guided visit about 45 minutes, passes every 40 minutes. Combined ticket with Busot’s ethnic music museum. Recent prices: adults €12, children 5–16 €5, under-fives free. From September to June they close on Mondays; in summer they open every day. Last ticket is earlier than you think. Phone from 11:00 on 615 171 485.

Cool even in August, which is why it works as a heat escape as much as a rain plan. The road up is fine in a normal car. Not a pushchair place.

Hours and tickets:[ Turismo Busot](https://turismobusot.com/que-ver/las-cuevas-del-canelobre/).

**Cova de les Calaveres, Benidoleig - 35 to 40 minutes**

Closer to the Dénia / Orba side of life. A cave with a long human story — Palaeolithic refuge, later uses, the slightly grim name (“cave of the skulls”). Good half-morning if you are already north of the Montgó and do not want Alicante traffic. Always check seasonal hours. Smaller sites here keep village timetables.

**MARQ in Alicante, and the Elche palaeontology museum**

If you have a child who likes bones, pots or “is that a real sword?”, MARQ in Alicante is the grown-up archaeology museum and Elche’s palaeontology museum is the dinosaur card. Both are proper indoor hours. I would not drag a two-year-old around either. I would happily take an eight-year-old on a wet Sunday.

Altea note, because people still ask: there is no little costume museum sitting in Altea town waiting for a rainy Tuesday. What Altea does have is a compact old town with overhangs, ceramic shops and bars you can sit in until the shower passes. If you want fiesta costumes, time it for late September when the Moros i Cristians filaes are actually in the streets. That is the real show.

**Cinemas that still feel like cinemas**

***Cine Jayan, Jávea*.** The village cinema. Family-run since the seventies, original version as a point of pride, one of the last big-screen rooms in the Marina Alta that has not been flattened into a bowling-and-smoothie shed. Tuesday is spectator day,  around €5 when they last published it. They mix family titles, VOSE and the odd special cycle. Carrer Santíssim Crist del Mar 14. Check the day’s version before you promise English.

***Cines IMF, Portal de la Marina*.** The convenient one. Monday and Wednesday cheap seats. Park once, film, dinner, home.

***Kinépolis, Plaza Mar 2*.** The big one. VOSE often in the mix.

***Cines Colci, Benidorm*.** Several rooms on Avenida de los Almendros and a second site at Rincón. Fine if you are already in Benidorm. 

**If the adults need the indoor plan**

Hotel spas with heated indoor pools exist in Benidorm, Calpe and Jávea. Some Calpe hotels run family water hours; most spa floors are 16+ after a certain time of day. Treat this as a parents-and-teens option, not a toddler solution. Book the slot. Turning up with three children and a bucket of hope is how you get a polite no at the towel desk.

Covered browsing: Altea old town for ceramics and jewellery, Moraira’s church-street stretch if you want a smaller, prettier wander, Dénia’s port arcades if you want lunch with a roof. Benidorm’s indoor market will keep you dry and busy. None of these replaces a play centre. They fill the hour after one.

**Two ready-made wet days**

***The “we are staying in Calpe” day.***

Morning: WhatsApp J-Park, or book Another World if the kids are older. Lunch under a roof in the port. Afternoon: Portal de la Marina,  shops, cinema spectator-day tickets if it is Monday or Wednesday, Carrefour for tomorrow’s breakfast. Home before anyone has to drive the N-332 in the dark and the spray.

**Using The Car**

Valor at the 10:00 English tour if you can get the slot. Painted houses in Villajoyosa if it is only drizzling. Then either Airjump in Finestrat on the way back, or push on to Plaza Mar 2 and Kinépolis if the ages skew older. Chocolate can make us all feel better after all.

**The boring useful bit**

Apps: AEMET for the official forecast, Google Maps for the “is the CV-70 a queue” question. The[ TRAM](https://sunseasangria.com/blog/2026-05-16-the-route-alicante--benidorm--denia/) will get you to Benidorm and Alicante from along the coast, but with buggies, wet coats and a cinema deadline, the car is kinder.

Pack a spare top each, a plastic bag for wet shoes, grip socks if you are heading to a trampoline park, and cash for the smaller play centres that still prefer it. Sunday hours are the ones that catch people out; caves, Valor and some play centres shut or go short.

And the thing I said at the start: rain here is often brief. If AEMET shows a hole at four o’clock, use it. Altea’s cobbles in a light shower look like someone turned the saturation up. Moraira harbour with a hot chocolate after is not a consolation prize. It is one of the better hours of the week.

**It's Your Turn**

If I have missed the indoor spot your family swears by, a play café that opened last month, a bowling alley I have not been to since the last time I wore dry shoes, tell me in the comments or reply to the Friday mail. This coast changes under our feet. The list should too.

Now if you will excuse me, I am going to look at the sky over the Peñón and decide whether this is a walking afternoon or a “put the kettle on and wait twenty minutes” afternoon.

With sun, sea and a decent roof,

Joanne x
