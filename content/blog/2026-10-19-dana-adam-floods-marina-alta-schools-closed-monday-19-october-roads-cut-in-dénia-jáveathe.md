Hola Terrace Crew,

Monday morning and the story is still the rain. DANA Adam has turned the whole Marina Alta into something much wetter and more serious than the usual October shower. AEMET kept the red alert running until 10 this morning; from there it has eased to yellow, but the ground is already saturated and the barrancos are doing what they do when they’ve had too much rain, please be  careful out there today.

By Sunday night the numbers had climber. Xàbia (Jávea) stations were well past 200 litres and, in places, pushing toward 270 for the day. Dénia was over 200. Further inland, around Pego and Forna, some gauges have gone past 500–600 litres over the last day and a half, figures not seen in this amounts of rain since the late 1980s. Streets flooded, cars got stranded (especially in Xàbia, where emergency services were pulling people out of vehicles), and the phones kept lighting up with ES-Alerts telling everyone to stay clear of rivers, barrancos, seafronts and low ground. The Generalitat put Marina Alta (and the Safor next door) into emergency level 1.

Today the practical consequence is that schools are closed right across the area. Dénia, Xàbia, Calp, Benissa, Pego, Pedreguer, Gata de Gorgos, Els Poblets, El Verger, Teulada-Moraira, Ondara and several others have cancelled classes and most municipal activity so families aren’t out on the roads while the alert is still active and accesses are being checked. Roughly 21,000 pupils in the Alicante side of things are staying home. Parks, the castle, museums and the cemetery in Dénia remain shut; the Fira de Tots Sants at Torrecremada stayed closed after its early finish on Saturday.

Roads are the other headache. Dénia still has its list of local closures (Sant Andreu, Camí de la Bota, Camí del Llavador, Camí Fondo, Marineta Cassiana, l’Alberca and others). The N-332 between roughly Gata de Gorgos and Pedreguer was partially flooded and only passable with caution late Sunday, the usual works around km 184–186 in Dénia are still there too, but the water is the bigger issue. Rough seas continue to batter the coast. Nothing dramatic if you stayed high and dry in the centre of town, but the low spots, underpasses and anything near a barranco are doing exactly what you’d expect.

That runner who got stuck on the Montgó overnight Saturday into Sunday is a reminder: he left his car on Calle Fenas, got caught on the slippery cuts in the dark and rain, and spent the night on the rocks until firefighters found him just after 8 a.m. and the helicopter lifted him off once it was light. Soaked and cold, but otherwise fine. Not the morning for a dawn circuit workout.

Down in Calpe the Moros y Cristianos are still scheduled for 21–26 October (their 50th year, now a Fiesta of National Tourist Interest). The programme starts Wednesday with the band entry and pregón; the big desembarco and first battle are set for Sunday morning on Arenal-Bol if the weather finally settles. Worth knowing if you were hoping for a quiet week.

Practical line for the rest of today: skip non-essential drives, especially anything low-lying in Dénia, near a barranco, or along the seafront. Check before you move and don’t assume the usual parking spots are usable. If the rain keeps easing and the yellow alert holds, things should improve through the day, but the soil is full and the drains are working hard.
