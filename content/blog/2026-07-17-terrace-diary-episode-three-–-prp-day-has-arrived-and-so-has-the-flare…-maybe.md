Hola Terrace Crew,

Pull up a lounger and grab something cold beacuse it’s very humid and hot out there.

If you read Episode One (or Two — I’m losing track already), you’ll know I finally got the MRI results confirming moderate osteoarthritis in my left hip. Grade 3 cartilage wear, that lovely bone marrow edema, and the pubic symphysis throwing its own little party. At 49, on the beautiful Costa Blanca, I decided it was time to try something before we start talking full hip replacement talk.

So here we are: **PRP injection talks**. Platelet-Rich Plasma — my own blood, spun and concentrated, then injected back into the troublemaker hip. Regenerative medicine, Spanish sunshine edition. My doctor has convinced me that this is best way forward and once again, he has said he doesn’t want to give me a hip operation at my age, that’s nice because he makes me younger than I am lol. so Monday at 4.30 I’m checked in and a low bank balance to boot, hope it’s worth it. 

**The Monday Morning Nerves**

I woke up nervous but weirdly optimistic. The terrace was already warm, Missy was doing her usual “why aren’t we walking yet?” dance, and I tried not to overthink the needle part. Hydrated like they told me, light breakfast, comfortable clothes. Drove to the clinic with my husband, windows down and some gentle music on — because apparently I’m now the type of person who has a “treatment playlist.” the thought of a painful injection is worrying me. 

The procedure itself? Quicker than I expected. Blood draw (easy), waiting while they processed it, then the guided injection. Local numbing helped, but yes — there was a solid few seconds of “okay, that’s deep” pressure. The doctor was brilliant, calm, and explained everything. In and out in under an hour.

**Right Now (A Few Hours Later)**

I’m back on the terrace with my feet up, ice pack on rotation, and a very understanding dog curled up nearby. The hip feels… grumpy. Not screaming pain, but definitely a deeper ache and some swelling — the famous PRP flare is making itself known. They warned me this could happen for a few days to a couple of weeks as the healing response kicks in.

I’m sticking to strict rest today and tomorrow: no long walks, no dramatic terrace dancing (yet), lots of gentle movement, and acetaminophen if needed. No NSAIDs for now — they apparently interfere with what the PRP is trying to do.

**The Hopeful Bit**

This is me choosing to give my body a fighting chance to repair before things get worse. The goal isn’t a miracle overnight — it’s buying time, reducing pain, and maybe improving the joint environment so I can keep enjoying this life I love: sunset terraces, gentle beach strolls, and chasing the next adventure (even if it’s at a slower pace these days).

I’ll be updating you honestly — the good days, the flare days, and whatever wins (or lessons) come along. Physio starts soon too, so the full team effort is underway.

To anyone else navigating OA, chronic pain, or just the general “body starting to complain in mid-life”: you’re not alone. Whether you’re in Spain, back in the UK, or somewhere else entirely — sending you a virtual sangria (non-alcoholic today) and a big virtual hug.

Drop a comment if you’ve tried PRP or any regenerative treatments — I’d love to hear your experiences.

Until Episode Four… rest, ice, elevate, and keep the terrace vibes alive.

With love and a slightly sore hip,

**Joanne xx** 🌴🍹🐾
