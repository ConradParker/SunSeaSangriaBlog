Hola Terrace Crew! ☀️🍷

Pull up a lounger. It’s Monday, 31 August, the official last day of the DGT’s Operación Retorno, and the coast is doing that familiar late-summer suitcases on pavements. Hire cars heading north, a slightly stunned quiet starting to settle over the beaches. August is packing its bags. September is already hovering in the doorway.

*Here’s what actually happened along our stretch last week, from Alicante up to Dénia.*

**Watch the roads today**

This is the one to take seriously.

The last special traffic operation of summer 2026 runs from Friday 28 August through midnight tonight. Across Spain, that’s nearly 7 million long-distance journeys. In the Comunitat Valenciana alone, Tráfico expected more than 908,000 movements.

On our roads, that means the usual pinch points: the AP-7 / A-7 corridor, the A-70 around Alicante, and the N-332 through Altea and the coastal accesses. Sunday afternoon into evening is usually the worst of the return. Monday morning still snarls the short hops between beach towns, then another wave heads inland later in the day.

Leave extra time. Don’t assume the N-332 “will be fine now the tourists are going.” They’re all going at once. Phone down. Distance up. If you can wait until after dark, do.

**The week the sea reminded us who’s in charge**

*A sad one first, because it matters.*

On Friday a 70-year-old man died after jumping into the sea from the rocks at Cala del Trampolí in Dénia. Early reports say he struck his head on a rock below the surface. Those jumps look harmless until they aren’t.

Further down the coast, a 21-year-old was cut by a propeller at Cala del Llebeig (the cove shared by El Poble Nou de Benitatxell and Moraira). The boat had been rented in Calpe. He was treated at Moraira port and taken to Hospital de Dénia.

In Calpe, La Fossa had a scare on Wednesday when a small tornado whipped across the sand, and an umbrella hit a child. Thankfully it was a minor injury, but it’s the sort of five-second chaos August specialises in.

And still hanging over the week: that strange sea last weekend. The *rissaga* / meteotsunami effect,  water dropping and surging in minutes, was felt in Xàbia and Dénia, with reports of waves pushing a long way inland in spots along the wider coast. If the sea looks “wrong,” get out of it. Flags are not decoration.

**Calpe & the hills**

A 12-year-old girl was found safe after going missing overnight on the Serra d’Oltà. Rescues like that are a reminder that our pretty little mountains are still mountains, even in August.

La Fossa and Arenal-Bol are shifting from peak-beach mode into that softer September rhythm. Zumba on the sand runs into late September. The Bofill building tours and Banys de la Reina visits were still ticking through August for anyone who wanted a dose of Calpe that isn’t a sunbed.

**Jávea / Xàbia**

The Mare de Déu de Loreto fiestas are in that lovely in-between: port still busy, boats, music, and the run-up to the early-September finale. If you’re heading to the port this week, expect local traffic mixed with the last holidaymakers, not full August gridlock, not winter quiet either.

Property headlines keep circling Xàbia. Along with Teulada-Moraira, Dénia and Calpe, it’s sitting at the top of the “most expensive in the province” lists again. None of that changes the Tuesday market. It does change the conversations over coffee.

**Dénia**

Alongside the beach tragedy, there was something quietly wonderful: two loggerhead turtle nests hatched at the same time at Les Albaranes, more than 140 hatchlings in one night. Dénia has had a remarkable turtle summer. Hold that against the harder news. Both belong to this coast.

Gent de Dénia has been waving a flag about the state of Monte Pego, potholes, upkeep, fire risk on those forest-edge urbanisations. Not glamorous. Very “real September” when the holiday lets are gone, and the year-round problems show again.

On the weather front, Dénia finally got its coolest day since June. The sticky midnights are loosening. Not autumn. Just a pause.

**Altea & Benidorm side**

Altea is lining up September: patronales and Moros y Cristianos later in the month, plus practical stuff, back-to-school help of €175 per child under 16, and extra support for children with additional needs. About 500 children wrapped up the summer sports courses at the Palau dels Esports.

Benidorm Pride was closing out its last big weekend, with tens of thousands still in town. If you drove the N-332 south at all last week, you felt it.

**Alicante city & the wider province**

The story that jumped the local papers was the overnight theft of the Tesoro de Villena, Bronze Age gold, valued in the millions, taken from the MUVI in minutes. Guardia Civil is on it. It’s inland, but it’s the province’s biggest headline of the week.

Also in Alicante: a fugitive wanted over a supermarket alcohol theft in Austria was picked up in the city. And Idealista numbers out this morning again put foreign buyers- Dutch, British, German- leaning hard toward Moraira, Benissa and the northern towns.

**The Great Escape**

You can see it without a statistic. Fewer inflatables. More roof boxes. Mercadona suddenly has your yoghurt again. The people who live here start reclaiming tables. The people who don’t are already on the AP-7 with a boot full of damp towels and a ferry or a flight in their heads.

The heat is not packing up with them. Not this week. Nights are still hanging around 24°C, the sort that keep the fan on and the sheets kicked off, and inland daytime temperatures can still climb to 35°C.

The sea is warm. The roads are busy. The fiestas are changing gear. And a few stories this week are a quiet reminder to be careful with water, rocks and rented boats.

If you’re driving today: slow down and let the return pass.

If you’re staying: the terrace is still yours. The evenings just haven’t cooled down yet.
