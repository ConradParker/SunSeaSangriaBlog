Hey there, fellow Costa Blanca expats! Let’s chat about renewing your **TIE (Tarjeta de Identidad de Extranjero)** — that essential plastic proof you’re legally settled in Spain.

* As of 2026, things are a touch more digital thanks to the updated Reglamento de Extranjería (since mid-2025), but the core remains the same: snag a cita previa, get your fingerprints done, and collect your fresh card after the wait.

You can start renewal 60 days before expiry or up to 90 days after (though don’t push your luck — fines or extra hassle could follow). For many Brits under the Withdrawal Agreement (swapping a 5-year card for permanent residency), it’s straightforward: no full re-application, just a quick fingerprint appointment at the police station.

Gather these bits:

* Your expiring TIE and passport
* Form **EX-23** (preferred for Withdrawal Agreement folks; EX-17 works for others)
* A recent biometric photo (proper size, no selfies!)
* Proof of tasa payment (Modelo 790-012, usually €12–20)
* Updated padrón certificate if you’ve moved house (you can print this, using your digital ID)

Book your cita previa online via the **Sede Electrónica de la Policía Nacional** — slots disappear quicker than free tapas, so try early mornings, refresh often and set alerts. If you’ve got a certificado digital or Cl@ve, some renewals (like non-lucrative or student) can kick off via **Mercurio** for extra ease.

Once approved, head back in about a month (sometimes longer) to pick up your new TIE — often now valid for 10 years if permanent. Handy tip: Your resguardo (receipt) plus old TIE keeps you legal in Spain while waiting, but grab an Autorización de Regreso if travelling abroad.

Not as daunting as it seems, eh?

 **Rules can vary by province (and your residency type), so always double-check locally or on official sites**. 

Here’s to smoother renewals and more sunny adventures with your updated card in pocket! 🌞🍷
