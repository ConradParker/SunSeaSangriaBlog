Hola Terrace Crew,

It’s one of those sticky Mondays when the air still feels like yesterday’s heatwave never quite left… and my car has decided to stage a full-blown breakdown. Garage appointment booked, no wheels for a week, so I’m firmly stuck on the sofa with a large coffee and the local news.

The roads are packed with people heading home or still clinging to their holidays, and the Costa Blanca North has thrown us the usual mix of tragedy, literal fireworks, fiestas and the odd bit of good news. **Weather first – keep an eye on the sky today**

AEMET has issued an **orange alert** for inland Alicante this afternoon (roughly 14:00–22:00) for intense storms, large hail and strong wind gusts. The coastal strip (Benidorm, Calpe, Dénia and the rest of us) is on yellow. Up to 40 mm of rain in an hour is possible inland, with the coast expecting less but still some drama. After the recent torrid nights (Dénia sensors hit 35 °C past 11 pm at one point), a proper storm might actually be welcome — don’t leave the sunbeds out.

*The weekend’s hardest news*

**N-332, Benissa (km 177, between Benissa and Calp)**

Saturday morning around 11:45–11:50, a head-on collision between two cars killed a 65-year-old man (the driver of one vehicle) and a 19-year-old young woman (passenger in the other). A 27-year-old and a 9-year-old child were injured and taken to Hospital de Dénia. Four people were trapped; firefighters from Benissa and Dénia freed them. A doctor and nurse who happened to be nearby gave the first assistance before emergency services arrived. The Guardia Civil is investigating. That stretch of the N-332 remains one of the more unforgiving bits of road in the comarca — especially with summer traffic.

Almost at the same time, the **AP-7** between Ondara and El Verger saw a van hit the barrier. No serious injuries reported there, but the combination of the accident and the usual Saturday rush of tourists heading to Dénia and Xàbia beaches caused solid tailbacks in both directions.

**Altea – the Castell de l’Olla goes wrong**

The big sea-launched fireworks display (Castell de l’Olla) on the night of 8–9 August drew the usual huge crowds. Early in the show, a shell went off course and landed on the beach among spectators (near the area of El Cranc). Twenty-seven people were injured — most with contusions from the sudden scramble to get clear, some with burns. Fourteen were taken to hospital initially; eleven ended up in burns units (mainly Alicante General). Most of the rest were treated at Altea health centre and discharged. One person was still reported in hospital in the latest updates. The organisers and the pyrotechnics company have expressed regret and are investigating. A spectacular night that should have been pure celebration ended with an ugly reminder of how quickly things can go wrong.

**Calpe – fiestas, fancy dress and more to come**

Calpe closed its **Virgen de las Nieves** programme with a lively Grand Parade full of floats, music and some wonderfully inventive children’s fancy dress (rubber rings, bath hats and all sorts). The Bous al Carrer are still running on Calle Conde de Altea through this week. Looking ahead, the **Calpe Pop Festival** (13–15 August) in the Parc de la Creativitat brings Kiko Veneno, Marta Santos, La Cabra Mecánica and a Queen tribute act. The town has also been dealing with a cleaning-strike headache — the mayor has publicly complained about minimum-service failures by the contractor.

On a brighter note, Calpe’s unemployment rate was recently recorded at its lowest in twenty years (just under 7 % in the June figures), which is no small achievement in high season.

**Moraira / Teulada**

El Portet beach was temporarily closed to swimming after elevated microbiological levels were detected. The red flag went up on Friday; by the weekend, the latest tests confirmed the water was safe again, and it reopened. A sensible, quick response. (There had been a similar short closure at Cala de l’Andragó earlier.)

### **Inland & the wider Marina Alta**

* A Guardia Civil operation in the comarca (triggered by a neighbour’s tip-off about suspicious rubbish bags and the smell of marijuana in the Pego area) dismantled two indoor grow-ops. Four people were arrested, and 265 plants seized, along with a firearm and cash.
* The extreme overnight heat has been the talk of every village from Benissa and Pedreguer up through the valleys — those 26–35 °C midnight readings are brutal even by our standards.
* Positive health news: the Dénia health department has created the first specialist paediatric nurse post for the area — a small but meaningful step for local families.

**Looking further south (Alicante–Benidorm border)**

Benidorm continues its usual summer mix: plans for a major new €50 m promenade linking the two bays are moving forward with an international design competition, while police have been dealing with the usual opportunistic thefts (including a man targeting hotel breakfast buffets) and the odd more unusual find (an urn of ashes left in a supermarket locker was eventually reunited with its owner).

**Coming up**

Dénia’s **Moors and Christians** main events kick into higher gear mid-month, so expect the historic centre and port to fill with colour, bands and pageantry. Xàbia is keeping a close eye on its posidonia meadows and has stepped up surveillance against boats dropping anchors on the protected seagrass.

That’s the week that was, Terrace Crew. a fireworks scare, a few closed beaches that reopened, fiestas that still managed to sparkle, and the roads doing what they always do in August. Drive carefully, check the sky this afternoon, and look after each other.
